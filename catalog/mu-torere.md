**FOUR MEN EACH. ONE EMPTY POINT. NOWHERE TO HIDE.**

An eight-pointed star drawn round a centre, like an octopus. You have four men, your opponent has four, and there's always exactly one empty point. Move a man into it, if it's joined. Nothing is ever taken: the side that can't move has lost. It sounds simple, and it's a knife-edge.

Mū tōrere was played on the East Coast of New Zealand's North Island, on a star marked in charcoal on a plank or on bark, sometimes just scratched in the earth, and written down in 1925 from Tuta Nihoniho of Ngāti Porou.

- **The pūtahi beside an enemy**: today's common rule; a man may step into the centre only from beside an enemy man
- **Mū tōrere after Best**: the rule as first written, where it holds only for the opening move, and the side that moves second can win at once; the game shows you how

The game is small enough to be solved, and the program solves it when it starts: on hard, the computer plays perfectly.

Play the computer at three strengths, from either side, or a friend at the same keyboard.

## Controls

- **Arrows**: move the cursor; **Enter** or **Space**: move the man under it; the men that may move are lit
- **Mouse**: click a man
- **F1** or **?**: the rules and where they come from; **M**: menu; **Q**: quit

## Options

- `--demo`: the computer plays both sides
- `--seed N`: the same game every time
