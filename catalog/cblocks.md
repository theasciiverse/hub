**THE PIANO HAS TO GET TO THE OTHER SIDE OF THE ROOM.**

The room is full of furniture. Nothing can be lifted. Nothing can be turned. Everything can only slide into the gap that's left. That was Dad's Puzzler, the best-known guise of a puzzle first sold in 1909, its big block a grand piano that had to cross a crowded room. Simple? Try it. And it's only puzzle number four.

cblocks is a tray of sliding blocks and a goal. Slide them until they stand where the puzzle wants them. Some ask you to march the biggest block from one end to the other; some want two identical blocks swapped; some want every block in its place. The rules take ten seconds. The solutions can take all evening.

- **Twelve classics,** from H. E. Dudeney's Motor-Car Problem (1903) and Sam Loyd's Moving Day Puzzle (1914), through L'Ane Rouge, the Red Donkey, to John Conway's Century-and-a-Half (1982), and more can be added as text files.
- **A best to beat:** every puzzle has a known shortest solution, counted in steps (a step is any number of slides of one block). The Motor-Car Problem takes 17. Conway's monster takes 148. How close can you get?
- **Your best is kept:** a new or better solution is saved, and loads back as redo when you return to the puzzle.
- **Undo and redo** slide by slide or step by step, save positions as you go, and peek at the goal whenever you need it.

**SLIDE. THINK. SLIDE AGAIN.**

## Controls

- **Arrow keys**: move the selection square by square
- **h j k l**: select the next block in that direction
- **H J K L**: slide the selected block; or drag a block with the mouse
- **g**: show the goal
- **x** / **z**: undo / redo a slide; **X** / **Z**: undo / redo a step
- **R**: start again; **s** / **r**: save / restore a position
- **N** / **P**: next / previous puzzle
- **?**: help; **q**: quit
