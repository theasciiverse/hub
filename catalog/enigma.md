**DARKNESS FALLS. TAKE EVERY PIECE OF GOLD, AND DON'T LOOK UP.**

The first level is called Darkness Falls, and what falls is arrows. Every `v` on the board drops the moment the space beneath it opens, and keeps on dropping, straight through soft earth, until something stops it. If that something is you, the level is over. Every `>` and `<` slides sideways the instant it can, and every `^` rises.

You are the `@`. Dig through earth, push arrows, sacks and bombs aside, and collect the `$`: sixty-one pieces on the first level alone. The exit only opens when the last one is yours. So every step is a question. Dig out the earth under a column of arrows and they come down, on you if you are below. Push an arrow off a ledge and it drops out of your way; push it the wrong way and a corner of the board is walled off for good. Bombs move like arrows and blow up when an arrow strikes them. Some levels give you a second self to swap to, and teleporters that come in pairs.

- **Twelve levels**, from Darkness Falls and Lateral Thinking through House of Cards to Devil's Despair and Shrouded Glory, each opening when you finish the one before
- **Ten save slots**, and you can restore one even after you have been crushed
- **Solutions as films**: write down your moves, load someone else's, and step through them forwards and backwards

## Controls

- **Arrow keys** or **h j k l**: move, dig, push
- **x**: swap to your other self
- **s**: save (pick a slot, then **y**), **r**: restore
- **w**: write your moves to a file, **m**: load a move file and watch it
- **q**: leave the level
- On the menu: **Up**, **Down** and **Enter** to play a level, **1** to **0** and **r** to resume a save, **q** to quit

## Options

- `enigma <set>`: play another level set from the `levels` folder

## History

Simon Tatham, the author of PuTTY, wrote this Enigma for Unix terminals in August 2000 and kept improving it until version 1.04 in February 2004, adding saved positions, move films, teleporters, a second player piece and the pieces needed to play XOR's levels. The rules and the twelve original levels came from a friend of his, who wishes to remain anonymous and gave the levels to the public domain. Chroma, also in the catalog, plays the same twelve levels as its third set.
