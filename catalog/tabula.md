**THE GAME THE EMPEROR LOST.**

Fifteen men each, three dice, and a race round the board and off it. Each die moves one man. Two of your men together close a point; a single man is hit when an enemy lands on him, and must start all over again. The first to bear off all fifteen wins. If that sounds like backgammon, it should: this is its Roman ancestor.

The Romans left boards by the hundred, dice towers, a tavern wall at Pompeii showing two players quarrelling over a throw, and exactly one recorded game: a poem tells how the emperor Zeno lost with a throw of 2, 5 and 6. Press F1 and see for yourself: the program sets up Zeno's board, and the poet was right. That throw leaves Zeno exactly one play, and it leaves eight of his men standing alone.

- **Tabula, the colours opposite**: the sides run against each other, as in backgammon
- **Tabula, the colours the same way**: both run the same way round, so every point is contested from the start
- **Ludus duodecim scriptorum**: the older game of three rows of twelve

Play the computer at three strengths, from either side, or a friend at the same keyboard.

## Controls

- **Enter** or **Space**: throw; then arrows to a lit man, **Enter** to pick it up, a lit point to put it down, one die at a time; **Esc**: let go, or take back the last step
- **Mouse**: click
- **F1** or **?**: the rules and where they come from; **M**: menu; **Q**: quit
- **O**: the braille ornament on or off

## Options

- `--demo`: the computer plays both sides
- `--seed N`: the same game every time
- `--plain`: start without the ornament, for fonts that draw braille thin
