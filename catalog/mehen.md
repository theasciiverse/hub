**RACE YOUR PHARAOHS UP THE COILED SNAKE. MIND THE LIONS.**

Five thousand years ago, Egyptians played a game on a snake: a serpent coiled round its own head, its body cut into squares, with little lions, lionesses and marbles. Then everyone stopped playing it, and the rules went with them. This is a 2024 reconstruction, built from the old boards, pieces and tomb pictures of clenched fists and open hands.

Your pharaoh is a marble at the snake's tail. Hide one to five marbles in your fist; your opponent guesses how many. They guess wrong: your pharaoh slithers forward that far. They guess right: theirs moves instead, and they may set a lion on yours!

A pharaoh held by a lion goes nowhere until the lion is dead, and killing a lion is a guessing game of its own: they hide one to three marbles, and one right guess kills it. Land on an enemy and you swap places. Two right guesses in a row bring a dead lion back. The first team to get every pharaoh to the head is reborn, and wins.

Play one against one, or teams of two or three. The computer bluffs with the mix game theory says is safest, and on hard it notices that you always hide five. Two people at one keyboard get a look-away screen while the other hides.

## Controls

- **1** to **5**, or **arrows** and **Enter**: hide or guess
- **Arrows** and **Enter**: choose a pharaoh
- **Y** (or **Enter**) or **N**: set a lion loose
- **Mouse**: click numbers, pharaohs and answers
- **F1** or **?**: the rules and what is known; **M**: menu; **Q**: quit
- **O**: the braille ornament on or off

## Options

- `--demo`: the computer plays both teams
- `--seed N`: the same choices every time
- `--plain`: start without the ornament, for fonts that draw braille thin
