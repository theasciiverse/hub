**ONE FOX. A WHOLE GAGGLE OF GEESE. SOMEBODY GETS EATEN.**

One fox, thirteen geese (or seventeen, if you like a crowd), and a board shaped like a plus sign.

The fox jumps geese like a draughts piece and takes them off the board, several in one move if they're careless. The geese can't take anything at all. All they can do is waddle, one step at a time, and close in until the fox has nowhere left to go.

Play the fox and it's a hunt: find the gap, wait for the slip, pick the gaggle apart. Play the geese and it's a siege: hold the line, never leave a goose with an empty point behind it, and squeeze. Thin the gaggle down to three and the fox wins, because three can never trap him. Hover a landing point and every goose the jump would eat turns red.

Hunt games like this are old in the north: a medieval saga mentions one, and an English king's household bought silver-gilt foxes and hounds for one in the 1460s.

- **Thirteen geese**: the older form; geese step any way along a line, and Bell swears that geese played right must win
- **Seventeen geese**: four more geese, but none may ever step back; wedge them in and the fox wins anyway

Play the computer at three strengths, as fox or geese, or a friend at the same keyboard.

## Controls

- **Arrows**: move the cursor
- **Enter**: pick up a piece, then put it down on a lit point; **Esc**: drop it
- **Mouse**: click a piece, then a point
- **F1** or **?**: the rules and where they come from; **M**: menu; **Q**: quit
- **O**: the braille ornament on or off

## Options

- `--demo`: the computer plays both sides
- `--seed N`: the same game every time
- `--plain`: start without the ornament, for fonts that draw braille thin
