**THE SHAPES KEEP FALLING. DON'T LET THEM REACH THE TOP.**

Four squares at a time, the shapes drop into the well. Slide them, spin
them, slot them into the gaps: fill a row from wall to wall and it
vanishes, and everything above it crashes down a line. Leave a hole and
it haunts you. Let the stack reach the top and it's over.

It only gets faster. Every level the shapes fall harder, at level nine
they come down nine squares a second, and the bravest players drop each
shape the moment it's lined up, because every square it falls on your
command is a point in your pocket. Your final score is multiplied by the
level you reached.

- **The next shape, shown in advance**: plan two moves ahead
- **Drop early, score more**: points for every row a shape falls when you
  send it down
- **Start where you dare**: pick a level from 1 to 9, or press **>** to
  speed up mid-game
- **A high score table** ranking every player by points and level

**One more row. Just one more.**

## Controls

- **Left** and **Right**, or **j** and **l**: move
- **Up** or **k**: turn a quarter counterclockwise
- **Space** or **Down**: drop
- **>**: go faster
- **p**: pause
- **q**, **F10** or **End**: quit

## Options

Started from a shell, `drop4 5` starts at level 5. Levels go from 1 to
9; at level n the shapes fall n times a second.

## History

This is BSD's tetris, which Chris Torek and Darren F. Provine adapted
from a winning 1989 entry to the International Obfuscated C Code
Contest (UC Berkeley, 1992); Hubert Feyrer added the preview of the next shape in 1999. In
2016 Mike Sharov renamed it Drop4 for his bsd-games and changed its
look: a wider well, no animation when rows clear, and the score and
next shape on the right. The high score table is kept per player.
