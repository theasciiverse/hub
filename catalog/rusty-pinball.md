**PULL THE PLUNGER. THE TABLE IS ALIVE.**

Real-time pinball physics, drawn in Braille vector lines right in your terminal. The ball rolls and ricochets off bumpers, slingshots and drop targets, the camera follows it up and down the table, and a dot-matrix display keeps the score. Light the lanes, raise the multiplier, lock the balls, and then hang on: here comes multiball!

Eight tables, each with its own rules. On Round and Round the bumpers orbit. On Eye of Horus a spinning triangle guards the pyramid at the centre. On Orbiters five planets circle a sun and tug at the ball as it passes. Ad Astra carries a map of the real night sky, 1,500 stars, and asks you to light up Orion, Leo or Ursa Major one star at a time. Pool Party racks them up and dares you to "Shoot the Eight Ball!" Ball locks, multiball and jackpots wait on every table that has them.

- **Eight tables**: Classic, Round and Round, Ringmaster, Compound Interest, Eye of Horus, Orbiters, Ad Astra and Pool Party
- **Every table plays by its own rules**: shoot Ringmaster's ramps and hit its bumpers while they glow red, match the ball's colour to the twelve points round the Eye of Horus, sink balls one to seven and then the eight
- **Real physics**: flippers, kickers, ramps, loops, and a whole mini table tucked inside Ad Astra
- **The original's sound**: Peter Drescher's score dings, rollover notes, and music that builds as you score
- **A safety net, as the original has it**: ball savers in the outlanes, a mercy ball for a quick drain, and lost balls replaced in the first moments of multiball
- **Your best five** on every table, and the last game's score
- **Settings** for music and effects apart, game speed, the whole table at once, and unlimited balls when you just want to play

## Controls

- **Space**: launch the ball; hold it to pull the plunger back for a softer or harder shot (on terminals with the kitty keyboard protocol)
- **Z**, **Left** or **Left Shift**: left flipper
- **X**, **Right** or **Right Shift**: right flipper (Shift works on terminals with the kitty keyboard protocol; elsewhere use the letters or arrows)
- **P**: pause
- **H**: help and the rules of the table in play
- **Esc**: back to the menu
- In the menu: **Up** or **k**, **Down** or **j**, **Enter** to choose, **Q** to quit; Settings, High scores and Help are below the tables

## History

Brian Nenninger set out in 2010 to make a pinball game for Android phones where the physics came first: no painted backglass, no flashy art, every bumper and wall drawn with plain lines and circles, and the ball moving as a real ball would. He called it Vector Pinball, shared its source openly, and has kept polishing it ever since, for more than fifteen years. Peter Drescher of Twittering Machine made its sounds and music, Jack Morgan designed the eighth table, Pool Party, and new tables kept arriving: the orbiting planets of Orbiters, then a whole night sky on Ad Astra.

Lines and circles, it turns out, are exactly what a terminal can draw. Rusty Pinball began in the summer of 2026 with Brian's table layouts and then went table by table through his source, bringing over each table's own rules, the moving parts, the locks, the jackpots and the sounds, until all eight tables played the way they do on a phone. Now the same ball rolls across the same tables, drawn in Braille dots.
