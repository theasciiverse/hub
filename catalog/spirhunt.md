The galaxy is overrun with space pirates, and you have limited time to
destroy them all. You command a warship with energy,
shields, plaser banks and ten torpedoes, and starbases where docking
restores your energy, shields and torpedoes. The pirates fight back, and so do
supernovas and black holes.

The screen always shows the sector you are in, your ship `Y` among
stars `*`, starbases `#`, pirates `P` and inhabited planets `@`, beside a
chart of the whole galaxy and a panel with your condition, energy,
torpedoes, the pirates left and the time remaining. You move by giving a
grid position: two digits for a spot in this sector, four to warp to
another.

## Controls

Each command is one key; the game then asks for what it needs.

- **m**: move, to a position such as `44` here or `3544` in another sector
- **I**: move under impulse, **w**: set the warp factor
- **p**: fire plasers, **t**: fire torpedoes, **R**: ram something
- **s**: shields up or down, **c**: cloak on or off
- **d**: dock or undock at a starbase, **H**: call a starbase for help
- **r**: rest, **i**: damage report
- **C**: ask a pirate to surrender
- **D**: self-destruct
- **?**: list the commands
- **S**: save and quit (the next start carries on), **Q**: quit at once

## History

Eric Allman wrote trek at UC Berkeley in 1976, and it shipped with BSD
Unix. Mike Sharov dropped it
from his bsd-games for its Star Trek content, then brought it back in
2020 as Space Pirate Hunt, with the same game and no Trek references.
Later that year he replaced its typed scan commands with a full-screen
curses display, moved navigation to a grid and simplified aiming.

The original teletype game, Klingons and all, is also in the catalog as
BSD Trek.
