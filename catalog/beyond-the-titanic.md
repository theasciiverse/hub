**"KKLLAAAAABBLLAAAANNGGGG!!!!!...The Titantic lurches sideways as a teeth-grinding sound rips across the side of the ship."**

It is 11:43 PM on April 14, 1912. A moment ago you were leaning on the starboard rail, counting stars over the icy sea while a wild party roared somewhere behind you, safe aboard the ship every passenger knew was unsinkable. Now the deck is tilting, the crowds are rushing for the boats, and you have very little time.

Make it into a lifeboat and the night is only beginning. A whirlpool drags you down into caverns under the ocean, where the ice hangs overhead like a ceiling and the wreck of the great ship rests above you. A wall of interlocking hexagons will open only for words chiselled elsewhere in the rock. Your flashlight will not last forever. Down a chasm, past an abandoned zoo with one cage standing open and empty, lies an alien saucer buried by a prehistoric rockfall, its pilot dead at the controls, its computer delighted to have company again, and something with three clawed arms loose in the cargo bay.

From there the way home runs through time itself: a future Earth of craters and crumbling concrete, hooded mutants who snatch what you carry, a building where doorways are made of orange light, and a shuttle ride up through the clouds to a city floating in the sky. There are 1000 points in it, and the game's own instructions reckon on more than 2500 moves for an average player. Draw a map.

## How to play

Type what you want to do and press Enter, in plain English. Several commands go on one line, joined by periods or THEN: **open hatch then climb down the ladder. read screen.**

- **north**, **south**, **east**, **west**, **up**, **down**, **in**, **out** and the diagonals (or **n**, **s**, **ne** and so on)
- **examine** (or **ex**) anything, **look** (or **l**), **inventory** (or **i**), **get** and **drop**, with **get all** and **drop all**
- **say** something, **type** on a keyboard, **turn** a dial to a number
- **diagnose** for your health and hunger, **score** for your rank, **wait** (or **z**), **repeat** (or **r**)
- **room description** (or **rd**) for short or full descriptions
- **save** and **restore** by name, **restart**, **quit**
- **Up** and **Down** bring back earlier commands; **Shift+PgUp** and **Shift+PgDn** page back through the story

Saved games live in `~/.local/state/beyond-the-titanic`.

## History

Scott Miller was writing about video games for The Dallas Morning News when he started a game of his own. On May 12, 1984 he began Beyond the Titanic in Turbo Pascal, a text adventure in the style of Infocom's Zork and Planetfall, with a vocabulary of more than 750 words. It came out in 1986 as shareware: copy it freely, and if you like it, send the author five or ten dollars by cheque, which would "encourage the author to make new and better games".

Not many cheques came. Miller took the lesson into his next idea, giving away the first part of a game and selling the rest, and in 1987 he founded Apogee on it with Kingdom of Kroz. Apogee went on to Commander Keen, Duke Nukem and Wolfenstein 3D, and sold Beyond the Titanic under its own name.

In March 1998 Apogee gave the game away as freeware. In 2009, while digging through Scott Miller's old floppy discs for the source of Kroz, they found this game's source too, and on March 20 released it as free software. It still only ran in an emulator, until 2014, when Jason Self spent about seventeen hours bringing the 1980s code forward to a modern compiler and played it through to the end.
