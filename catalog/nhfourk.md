**THE SAME DEADLY DUNGEON. A LESS HOSTILE INTERFACE.**

The Amulet of Yendor still lies at the bottom, past the Mazes of Menace, the Gnomish Mines, Sokoban and Gehennom, and the dungeon still wants you dead. What has changed is everything around it. NetHack 4 set out to give NetHack "a less hostile interface"; Nathan Eady's NetHack Fourk takes that and adds the balance changes NetHack 4 would not.

- **Autoexplore and travel**: **v** explores until something turns up, **_** walks you to any spot.
- **Status bars** for health and power, and **an inventory sidebar** on a wide terminal.
- **Every level remembered**: look back on any level you have seen.
- **Fewer ways to kill yourself with a typo.**
- **Rebalanced**: races matter for your limits and skills, Barbarians are the strongest fighters, ranged weapons and multishot reworked, amnesia gone. A new race, the sylph, and new levels and monster tricks.
- **Roles you earn**: you start as a human Barbarian or Wizard, and the rest unlock as you achieve things in play, and stay unlocked. The Archeologist? Win the game first.

It is the version played on the Hardfought public servers.

## Controls

- **hjkl yubn**, the cursor keys or the number pad: move, the last
  four letters diagonally; walking into a door opens it
- **F** and a direction: fight; **t**: throw, **f**: fire, **z**: zap
- **v**: explore automatically until something turns up
- **_**: travel to a spot (`<` or `>` there picks the stairs)
- **i**: inventory, **,**: pick up, **d**: drop
- **e** eat, **q** quaff, **r** read, **a** apply, **A** change your
  equipment (wield, wear, put on, take off, all in one)
- **s**: search, **.**: rest; a number first repeats, as in `20s`
- `>` and `<`: stairs down and up; **Ctrl-O**: the levels you remember
- **#**: extended commands, such as `#pray`; **Ctrl-E**: write Elbereth
- **?**: help, **;**: what is that, **Ctrl-P**: earlier messages,
  **!**: the game menu
- **S**: save and quit

## Saving

**S** saves the game to your data folder (`~/.local/share/nhfourk`);
pick it with **load game** in the main menu to carry on. Your options
(set from the main menu or with **O** in a game), the score list and a
full account of every finished game are kept there too.

## History

NetHack 4 began when Alex Smith merged his AceHack with Daniel Thaler's
NitroHack, two forks of NetHack 3.4.3, and rewrote much of the game's
insides and all of its interface while keeping the gameplay
conservative. Nathan Eady started NetHack Fourk from it in 2014 to try
out the balance changes NetHack 4 would not take, and still maintains
it; this edition is built for the terminal only, with its files in your
own data folder. NetHack itself is in the catalog too.
