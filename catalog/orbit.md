**IT'S UP THERE. YOU CAN'T SEE IT. YOU HAVE SEVEN HOURS.**

A cloaked raider is circling your planet. It runs counterclockwise, somewhere between 10,000 and 30,000 miles out, once every 12 to 36 hours, and in seven hours it will have the power to slip away into deep space.

You have one bomb an hour. Aim it by an angle and a distance, fire, and wait. You will never see the raider, but your instruments tell you how far from it the bomb burst. Draw the circle of everywhere it could have been. Fire again. Where the circles cross, it is hiding. Get a burst within 5,000 miles and it is torn apart. The planet is safe!

Miss, and seven hours later the truth is drawn in violet across the sky: the path it flew while you were guessing.

- **The plot from above:** your planet, the rings at 10,000, 20,000 and 30,000 miles, every burst with its circle of possible raiders, and your aim with its kill radius
- **Aim with the mouse** and click to fire, or set the numbers by hand
- **Pure deduction under the clock:** geometry, a little luck, and one shot an hour

## Controls

- **Mouse**: aim, click to fire
- **Tab**: angle or distance; **↑ ↓**: change it; typing replaces it
- **Enter**: fire; after the hunt, a new one
- **M**: sound on or off; **Esc**: quit

## History

Jeff Lederer wrote it as *Space War* at Project SOLO in Pittsburgh. The hidden ship and the distance after every miss made it a lesson in the law of cosines dressed as a space battle, and David H. Ahl printed it as *Orbit* in *BASIC Computer Games* (1978), with the geometry explained for anyone who wanted to cheat with a pencil. Here the pencil is built in: every circle is drawn for you, and the hunting is yours.
