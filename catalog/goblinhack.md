**THE GOBLINS ARE AWAKE. SO ARE THE GLORKS.**

Somewhere under your feet, a dungeon is filling up with things that want you dead, and they are not waiting their turn. Goblin Hack is a real-time dungeon romp: the moment you step off the stairs, everything moves at once. Spiders skitter, slime molds bubble, a hydra lifts all its heads, and you had better already be running, swinging or casting.

Pick your hero from a cast of twenty-one, from the Wizard and the Valkyrie to a Minotaur, a Necromancer, a Future Soldier with a gun, or a goblin of your own. Then go down: twisting dungeons, dripping caves and strange fractal forests, most of them made fresh each time, with shops, keys, traps and gold glinting in the dark.

And here is the magic trick. Neil McGill drew every creature in this game by hand, in characters: each orc, beholder and dragon is its own little ASCII picture with a face, frame after frame of animation. In the dungeon you see the classic roguelike view, one character a square, lit only by your hero's light. Press **Tab** and the world zooms in: every monster around you appears as the drawing Neil made for it, glaring right back at you.

- **Seventy kinds of monster**, from goblins, ghosts and vampires to medusas, liches, beholders, glorks, squarms, smurlingers, a pumpkin and, yes, a policeman
- **Twenty-one heroes**, each with their own strength, cunning and kit
- **Spells, wands and potions**, swords, axes and warhammers, bows and guns, armour and amulets
- **Shops**, and a shopkeeper who expects to be paid
- **Fate points** to drag you back from death, until they run out
- **The bones of fallen heroes**, left in the dungeon for the next adventurer to find

## Controls

- **Arrow keys** or the keypad: walk; **Space**: attack and fire
- **a** and **z**: choose an item; **u** or **Enter**: use it; **d**: drop it
- **o**: open doors; **p**: pause, or pay in a shop
- **Tab**: zoom into the pictures
- **s**: save; **h**: help; **q**: quit

## History

Neil McGill started Goblin Hack in 1999, a dungeon game in C++ where everything, walls and treasure and every last monster, was made of characters, then scrolled smoothly across the screen with OpenGL. He kept it alive for more than twenty years, even as he went on to make Zorbash and Sacrecant. In 2026 it came home to the terminal it was always drawn for: no window, no graphics card, just the characters.
