**THE GAME THAT BECAME LUDO, AS EMPERORS PLAYED IT.**

A cross of four arms, four colours of four men, partners sitting opposite. Each man leaves the middle (in chaupar they start out on the board), runs right round the cross and comes home again: 84 steps. Land on an enemy and he goes back to the start, unless he sits on a castle. A pair holds its square; but play Falkener's rules and a pair is at its own peril, and one man takes them both! The partnership with all eight men home first wins.

At the emperor Akbar's court, around 1590, a sitting of sixteen games could last three months, and a player who lost patience had to drink a cup of wine. The name comes from the best throw of the cowrie shells, twenty-five: pachis. The English took it home in the 1880s, and called it Ludo.

- **Pachisi after Tylor**: six cowries, the rules as recorded from Calcutta in 1879
- **Pachisi after Falkener**: the rules of 1892, as Bell reprinted them
- **Chaupar after Abu'l-Fazl**: three long dice and no castles, the game of Akbar's court

Play the computer at three strengths, from either partnership, or a friend at the same keyboard; each side plays both colours of its partnership.

## Controls

- **Enter** or **Space**: throw; then arrows to a lit man, **Enter** to pick it up, a lit square to put it down; men sharing a square can march on together; **Esc**: let go; **P**: leave the rest of the throws unused
- **Mouse**: click
- **F1** or **?**: the rules and where they come from; **M**: menu; **Q**: quit
- **O**: the braille ornament on or off

## Options

- `--demo`: the computer plays both sides
- `--seed N`: the same game every time
- `--plain`: start without the ornament, for fonts that draw braille thin
