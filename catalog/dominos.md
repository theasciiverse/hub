**IVORY TILES. GREEN BAIZE. CLACK! SHUFFLE UP!**

Pull up a chair. Your hand stands in front of you, and nobody else can see it. Match an open end and lay your tile down: clack! Go out first and the pips left in everyone else's hands are yours. Get caught holding the double six when the line blocks, and that's twelve more for somebody else.

Three games, two to four players, and nobody minds if you count on your fingers:

- **The Block game**: the purest one. Match an end or pass; the first to go out, or the lowest hand when everyone is stuck, scores from everyone else's pips
- **The Draw game**: the same, with a boneyard to draw from whenever you like, even when you could play, until only two tiles are left there. More tiles, more choices, more pips to be caught holding
- **All Fives**: the first double becomes the spinner and the line grows four arms. Make the open ends add up to five, ten, fifteen and score on the spot! First to 200 wins, the moment it's reached
- **Round the table on your local network**: open a table, your friends find it by themselves and take the seats, and the computer fills the rest. Everyone sees only their own hand. No peeking!
- **Partners at four**, sitting opposite and scoring as a side
- **A sly computer** at three strengths: in All Fives the hard one remembers which numbers you couldn't play, and leaves you ends you can't match

## Controls

- **← →** or **h l**: choose a tile from your hand, or click it
- **Enter** or **Space**: play it; where it fits two ways, **← →** choose the end
- **D**: draw from the boneyard
- **?** or **F1**: the rules of the game you are playing
- **Q** or **Esc**: back to the menu

## Options

- `--rules block|draw|fives`: the game the menu starts on
- `--demo`: the computer plays every seat
- `--seed N`: the same deal again

## History

In China, written mention of gaming tiles goes back to thirteenth-century Hangzhou. The dominoes of the West appeared in France in the middle of the eighteenth century; the name is found from 1762, most likely after the domino, the hooded carnival cloak and its mask. From France the game spread across Europe and reached England by the late 1700s, carried there, the story goes, by French prisoners of war, and the first English games were the two you find here first: the Block game and the Draw game. All Fives came in the nineteenth century, the first scoring game, borrowing its counting from cribbage. Asciiverse set all three out on green baize in 2026.
