**THINGS ARE FALLING. SHOOT THEM ON THE BEAT.**

Spheres, boxes, tumbling crates, green diamonds, cyan bonuses, the odd red mine, all of them dropping out of the sky toward a little grid below. You have two machine guns, a mine emitter and the mouse. Most of what reaches the grid explodes, and your health goes with it.

Short, precise bursts bring things down, and the ammunition runs out faster than you think. Shoot a crate for more rounds, or a blue one for rapid fire that turns your bullets red. Shoot a green diamond to patch your armour, a cyan one for two thousand points. Drop a mine into a crowd and hit it with a bullet: the blast takes everything near it. The longer you last, the faster it all falls, and a narrator tells you when your armour is running low.

Turn on **groove mode** and the music takes over. Every press of the trigger is judged against the beat, PERFECT or GOOD, and each one on the beat feeds a combo that doubles, triples, up to five times what your kills are worth. The waves stop falling at random and drop in on the beats instead, a bigger wave when the music swells into a loud bar, and the sound scope above your gauges flashes white with every kick. Miss four beats and the combo falls apart. Played well, it feels less like shooting and more like drumming with two machine guns.

And all of it is real 3D: a lit, shaded scene drawn many times a cell and turned into characters, ` .,:;=+tIYVRBM` from dark to bright, in the sixteen colours of the Windows console it was made for. Debris tumbles, blasts fade, guns recoil, and the aiming box follows your mouse with its scales and ammunition bars.

- **Seven tracks in the playlist**, from Juhani Junkala's chiptunes to remixes that build their drums as the game builds its pace
- **A Braille sound scope** tracing the game's own mix, music, gunfire and blasts together
- **Two Halls of Fame**, one for the classic game and one for the groove, where the author's own 100,000 is still waiting to be beaten
- **The original's switches**, from its sharpest antialiasing to twice the debris, and a male or female narrator

## Controls

- **Mouse:** aim; **left button** fires both machine guns, **right button** drops a mine
- **Arrows** aim, **Space** fires, **M** drops a mine
- **G** in the menu switches groove mode; **C** calibrates its timing (tap Space on the clicks)
- **S** shows or hides the sound scope; **Escape**: back to the menu

The original's letters still work after the command: `a` sharpest antialiasing, `n` none, `f` frames per second, `m` male narrator, `c` no debris, `p` twice the debris. `--truecolor` colours each character with its real colour instead of the sixteen, `--groove` starts in groove mode, and `--classic` gives the 2005 screen exactly.

## History

Jan Ringoš, known online as Tringi, made Shoot 'em in 2005 as freeware for the Windows XP console. Its trick was his own: draw a full OpenGL scene off-screen, then average every patch of pixels into one character and one of the console's sixteen colours, sixty times a second, so a text window showed a lit, moving 3D world.

Twenty years later the source was gone. Jan couldn't find it anywhere, and gave his blessing to rebuild the game from the program itself, decompiling it if needed. So it was taken apart instruction by instruction, every rule, number and screen written down, and rebuilt for today's terminals on Linux, macOS and Windows, his character ramp and sound effects kept exactly. His original release is the first thing in the new source, with his name on it.

Then it got a new soundtrack, and the music turned out to carry the guns so well that the game learned to listen to it: groove mode, new in 2026.
