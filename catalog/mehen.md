**RACE YOUR PHARAOHS UP THE COILED SERPENT AND BE REBORN! BUT MIND THE LIONS!**

Every night, the Egyptians told, the sun god Ra sailed through the underworld, and the great serpent Mehen coiled his body round him to keep him safe. Five thousand years ago they played a game on that serpent: a snake coiled round its own head, its body cut into squares, with carved lions and lionesses and a handful of marbles. A piece that crawled from the tail to the head, one scholar argued, was a soul passing through the body of the god, to be reborn.

Your pharaoh waits at the tail. Hide one to five marbles in your fist; your rival guesses how many. Wrong? Your pharaoh slithers forward that far. Right? Their pharaoh moves instead, and they may set a lion on yours! A pharaoh held by a lion goes nowhere until it kills the beast, and that is a guessing game of its own. Land on an enemy and you swap places. Guess right twice in a row and a dead lion comes back to life. The first team to bring every pharaoh to the serpent's head is reborn, and wins!

Bluff, double-bluff, and read your rival's face... or the computer's. It mixes its hiding the way game theory says is safest, and on hard it notices if you always hide five.

- **Seventy squares of coiled serpent**, from the tail to the hood
- **Lions and lionesses**: three cats a team, to set, to kill and to bring back
- **One against one, or teams of two or three**, as a relief from King Sahure's causeway shows three players on each side of the board
- **A look-away screen** when two people share the keyboard, so nobody sees the fist
- **The computer at three strengths**

## Controls

- **1** to **5**, or **arrows** and **Enter**: hide or guess
- **Arrows** and **Enter**: choose a pharaoh
- **Y** (or **Enter**) or **N**: set a lion loose
- **Mouse**: click numbers, pharaohs and answers
- **F1** or **?**: the rules and what is known; **M**: menu; **Q**: quit
- **O**: the braille ornament on or off

## Options

- `--demo`: the computer plays both teams
- `--seed N`: the same choices every time
- `--plain`: start without the ornament, for fonts that draw braille thin

## History

Mehen was played in Egypt from about 3000 BC until the end of the Old Kingdom, around 2200 BC, and then it vanished: no board or scene of it is known from the Middle or New Kingdom, though it lasted longer on Cyprus. What it left behind is tantalising. A painting in the tomb of Hesy at Saqqara lays out the board, three lions, three lionesses and the marbles. In the tomb of Kaemankh a player holds a closed fist up towards the board. A block from King Sahure's causeway, found in 2019, shows six people round the game under the words "Playing Mehen", and in front of one of them, perhaps, "Four!"

The rules were lost. In 2024 James Masters went back through every board, piece and picture, and from the clenched fists, the marbles just the right size to hide in a hand and the lions too big to travel the track, built the game you play here. The rim of the board carries the serpent's scales, after an alabaster mehen board now in Chicago.
