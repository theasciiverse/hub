**THE AZTEC GAME PLAYERS STAKED THEMSELVES ON.**

A cross painted on a mat, six stones each, and five black beans, each with a single white hole. Cast the beans: the holes showing count one to four, and all five count ten. Bring your stones in, take them once round the cross and off. The pinched squares cost you forfeits; the rounded ends give you another throw. The first with all six stones off wins the pool.

A Spanish friar in Mexico described patolli around 1579: the mat, the stones, the beans, the god of the beans, and the gamblers who, with nothing left, staked themselves, and became slaves for ever if they couldn't buy themselves back. The authorities tore up the mats and burned the beans, and nobody wrote the rules down. What you play here are the rules a games historian suggested in 1969, on the friar's equipment.

Your first throw picks the way round for every stone you own; one stone to a compartment, so nobody is ever taken.

Play the computer at three strengths, as Red or Blue, or a friend at the same keyboard.

## Controls

- **Enter** or **Space**: cast the beans; then arrows to a lit compartment, **Enter** to send a stone there; **B**: bear a stone off
- **Mouse**: click
- **F1** or **?**: the rules and where they come from; **M**: menu; **Q**: quit
- **O**: the braille ornament on or off

## Options

- `--demo`: the computer plays both sides
- `--seed N`: the same game every time
- `--plain`: start without the ornament, for fonts that draw braille thin
