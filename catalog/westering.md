**OREGON OR BUST! TWO THOUSAND MILES, FIVE OF YOU, ONE WAGON.**

Independence, Missouri, spring 1848. Somewhere past the prairie, the Platte, the Rockies, the Snake River desert and the Blue Mountains lies the Willamette Valley, green and waiting, and a new life for all five of you. Name your party, choose the month you leave, and spend your $700 at the store: oxen, provisions, clothing, powder and shot, and spare parts for a wagon that will break.

Then the road. Set the pace and the rations. Ford the Kansas River, pass Chimney Rock and Scotts Bluff, reach Independence Rock and cross the Rockies at South Pass. Stalk buffalo on the plains and elk in the mountains, creeping up from downwind with the light going. Trade dried salmon for needles and thread, find a camp with sweet water and wild currants, and rest when someone is failing, because the road is hard: cholera on the plains, mountain fever in the high country, a rattlesnake in the grass, an ox that lies down in the traces and does not get up.

And when one of you falls, you cut a board for the grave and choose what it shall say. Can you bring them all to Oregon City before the snows close the passes?

## The road

- **Twenty-five landmarks of the 1848 road,** from Chimney Rock and Independence Rock to South Pass, Fort Hall and the Blue Mountains, each drawn in half blocks with a note on what stood there that year
- **Hunting on foot:** the wind carries your scent, cover hides you, antelope see far and buffalo often stand while their neighbours fall
- **Seven rivers** to ford, float or ferry, running high with snowmelt in May and June
- **Two great forks:** the dry Sublette Cutoff or Fort Bridger; the Barlow Road over Mount Hood or a raft down the Columbia
- **Forts with trade rooms,** dearer the further west you go
- **Mind the calendar:** leave late and the first snows catch you on the high passes, the Rockies from September, the Blue Mountains and the Cascades from October
- **A register** of every journey you have made, and the best of them

## Controls

- Arrow keys to choose, Enter to do it, Esc to go back
- On the trail, at landmarks and at rivers the number keys pick at once
- Esc on the trail stops for now; the journey waits for you, and `c` on
  the title page takes it up again
- In the store, Right or `+` buys and Left or `-` puts back
- Stalking: the arrow keys creep (`q` `e` `z` `c` for the diagonals),
  Space fires at the nearest animal, Enter heads back to the wagon

## History

In the autumn of 1971 Don Rawitsch, a student teacher from Carleton College, was teaching an eighth-grade history unit on the westward expansion in Minneapolis. He planned a board game about the Oregon Trail; Bill Heinemann, a fellow student teacher, suggested a computer program instead, and with Paul Dillenberger they wrote *The Oregon Trail* in about two weeks for the school district's HP 2100, played on a single teleprinter. It met its first class on 3 December 1971, and generations of schoolchildren have taken the trail since. Westering is a journey of our own in that line, written in 2026: the real road of 1848, its forts and forks and rivers, the year before the great cholera years and the gold rush crowds.
