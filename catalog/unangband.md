**THE WHOLE OF MIDDLE-EARTH IS DOWN THE ROAD. SO IS THE ONE RING.**

You wake in Hobbiton with a hundred gold pieces and a road in front of you. Farmer Maggot has lost his dogs, Grip, Fang and Wolf, and the trap doors to his famous cellars stand open. Beyond the Shire lie the Old Forest and Tom Bombadil's house, the Barrow-downs, Bree and Weathertop, Rivendell, the mines of Moria, Mirkwood and the Lonely Mountain, Isengard, Edoras and Minas Tirith, and at the end of everything the Plains of Gorgoroth and the fire of Orodruin. Somewhere out there is the One Ring. If Sauron gets it first, Middle-earth is doomed.

Unangband is the Angband variant that tried to put everything in. Press **<** in a town or at the edge of the wild and choose where to go next; every place is its own wood, ruin or dungeon, with its own monsters and its own guardian at the bottom.

You can walk that road as almost anyone. A Hobbit, a Dwarf, a Dúnadan, a Man of Rohan or of Harad, an Ent, a giant of the forge, or, for the bold, an Orc, a Werewolf or a Vampire: more than twenty peoples in all. And as one of thirteen callings, from Warrior and Thief to Bard, Shaman and Istari, each with a style of its own to master. A Dwarf who trusts nothing but his hammer. An Archer who lives for the bow. A Warrior with a blade in each hand.

The world pushes back in ways the old dungeons never did. Rivers to cross, ice to slip on, lava to skirt, chasms to fall into, and rooms that describe themselves as your light reaches them. Everything interacts: set a potion in a trap, coat your arrows in it, or throw it like a grenade and watch the smoke roll. And there is always another quest, from Farmer Maggot's lost dogs all the way to the Crack of Doom.

## Controls

- **Arrow keys** or **1** to **9**: move; walk into a monster to attack it
- **<**: travel from a town, or from the edge of a wild place; **<** and **>**: stairs in a dungeon
- **g**: pick up, **i**: inventory, **e**: equipment, **w**: wear or wield
- **q** quaff, **r** read, **E** eat, **a** aim a wand, **u** use a staff, **z** zap a rod, **f** fire, **v** throw
- **m**: cast a spell, **b**: browse a book
- **y**: apply a rune or potion to something; **j**: set a trap
- **C**: character sheet, **l**: look around, **M**: map, **Ctrl-Q**: your quest
- **=**: options (a roguelike keyset is there too); **?**: help
- **Ctrl-S**: save, **Ctrl-X**: save and quit

## History

Andrew Doull released the first version in November 2000, a set of changes to Angband that did not yet have a name. Robert Ruehlmann, Angband's maintainer at the time, called it UnAngband for the moment, and the name stuck: the Unnamed Angband. Over the next decade Doull poured in new classes and fighting styles, hundreds of monsters, terrain, room descriptions and a whole map of Middle-earth to travel, taking code from Oangband, Sangband and NPPAngband and lending his terrain system back to NPPAngband, and he wrote about its design on his blog, Ascii Dreams. He carried it to 0.6.4c. In 2016 Duncan Lowther, the Gold Dragon, picked it up and put it on GitHub; Doull announced him as the new maintainer, and with dennis-roof he added the quest to carry the One Ring into Orodruin.
