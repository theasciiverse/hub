**A PAPER GLIDER. A WHOLE HOUSE. NOTHING HOLDING YOU UP BUT THE AIR.**

You are at the controls of a paper glider loose in a big old house, and
the way out is through every room of it. A glider only falls; what keeps
it up is the air from the floor vents, so you drift from vent to vent,
steering left and right and climbing to the ceiling, while the room does
its best to bring you down. "Foresight and daring are rewarded," as John
Calhoun put it in 1991.

He also confessed he had no intention of telling you what everything in
the rooms does: the floor, tables, shelves and books are most certainly
bad, the clocks most certainly good ("points, points, what's a game
without points?"), and you'll enjoy figuring out the rest. A few
freebies: candles lift you too, "but not too close!"; toast leaps from
the toaster, fish from their bowls; outlets spark, grease spills,
shredders wait. And through the air come the balloons ("slow and
harmless looking, but not party invitations!"), paper 'copters ("of
mysterious origin, but of dire design!") and the darts: "Fast and
unforgiving... Out of way!" Folded paper is an extra glider, a battery a
burst of speed, rubber bands your only defense. Ducts and stairs lead on,
switches bring back the lights, the air or a fan, and the faster you
cross a room, the bigger its time bonus.

Twelve houses come with it, from The House and Calhoun's own Mad House to
the fans' houses of the day: the 120 rooms of the House of the Rising
Sun, the Glass House with a window in every room, and Hands-off House,
which plays itself. Any Glider 4 house file plays too.

It is Calhoun's game, translated from his Pascal, with his art, sounds
and houses read from his own files. The silhouette view is made for the
terminal: the room softened behind box-drawn furniture, and everything
that moves or hurts drawn sharp in solid colour. Or press **V** for his
art itself, in half blocks.

**Catch the air. Mind the darts.**

## Controls

- **Left** and **Right** (or **,** and **.**): fly; let go and the glider
  just falls
- **Up** or **Enter**: burn the battery; **Space** or **Down**: fire a
  rubber band
- Mouse: point left or right of the room's middle to fly that way, as on
  the Mac; the button burns the battery
- Gamepad: d-pad or stick to fly, **A** fires, **B** or the right
  trigger boosts, **Start** pauses
- **Tab**: pause; **V**: the other view; **M**: sound; **B**: music;
  **Q**: end the game
- Type **Ozma** on the title screen for fifty gliders, as in the original

Best in a terminal that reports key releases (kitty, foot, WezTerm,
Ghostty and others). Elsewhere a tap sets the glider going that way, as
Glider's own tap control did; the mouse and a gamepad don't depend on it.

## Options

- `glider "Mad House"`: start in a built-in house; `--houses` lists them
- `glider path/to/house`: play a Glider 4 house file from disk
- `--watch`: no controls, for Hands-off House
- `--silhouette`, `--original`: which view to start in
- `--mute`: no sound

## History

Calhoun wrote Glider 4.0 for the Macintosh in THINK Pascal, and Casady &
Greene published it in 1991, "a wistful flight of fancy for those rainy
days". It came with a Room Editor, and players built houses of their own
and passed them around on America Online. Steve Sullivan offered $10 to
the first player to send him a screenshot of his House of the Rising Sun
finished, and soon after, Calhoun wrote asking to include it in a
collection of houses to be sold. Calhoun followed Glider 4 with Glider
PRO in 1994, and released Glider 4's source, art, sounds and houses
under the MIT licence in 2016.

This edition keeps his rules and his house files as they were. Three
timers in Hands-off House are moved, as it was tuned on a slower Mac, so
it still plays itself to the end.
