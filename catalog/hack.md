Hack is the dungeon crawl that NetHack grew from. You go down through the
Mazes of Menace, level by level, fighting what you meet, finding armour,
weapons, potions, scrolls, rings and wands, and learning what they do by
trying them. Somewhere far below lies the Amulet of Yendor. You choose to
be a Tourist, Speleologist, Fighter, Knight, Cave-man or Wizard, and your
little dog comes along.

Everything is a letter on the map: you are the `@`, monsters are letters
of their own, `#` is a corridor and `>` the stairs down. Death is final,
and the dead you leave behind may haunt the next player on that level.

## Controls

- **hjkl yubn**: move, the vi way (diagonals on yubn); with **Shift**,
  run
- **s**: search, **.**: rest, **>** and **<**: stairs
- **i**: inventory, **,**: pick up, **d**: drop
- **w**: wield, **W**: wear, **q**: quaff, **r**: read, **e**: eat,
  **z**: zap a wand, **t**: throw
- **?**: help, **/**: what is that symbol
- **S**: save and quit, **Q**: quit, **^R**: redraw

## History

Jay Fenlason wrote Hack in 1982, his first-semester programming
project, with Kenny Woodland, Mike Thome and Jon Payne, after the game
Rogue. Andries Brouwer then reworked it at the CWI in Amsterdam ("in fact an entirely
different game", says its manual), and that 1985 version, 1.0.3, was
shipped with BSD and became the base of NetHack.

NetBSD keeps the original game; Mike Sharov's bsd-games trimmed it in
2018 and later dropped it. The Asciiverse edition ports it to Linux and
macOS and keeps its record, saved games and bones in the player's own
data folder.
