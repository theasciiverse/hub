**NINE HOLES. ONE ISLAND GREEN. MIND THE WATER!**

Step up to the first tee at Gentle Start. Ahead lies a nine-hole course, par 36, seen from above: fairway and rough, a band of trees, sand traps, ponds, and a flag on every green. Pick your club from the driver to the putter, aim anywhere you like, and choose how hard to swing.

Then the course fights back. The wind blows its own way on every hole and carries your ball with it. Rough steals your distance, trees steal more, and a hard swing can hook or slice it into trouble. Splash! One stroke added. Out of bounds? Play it again. Make it to the green and the view closes in so you can read the slope before you putt.

Work your way round Mill Pond, Long Meadow, Dogleg Left and the dreaded Island Green, across Creek Crossing, along The Long Way Home and back to the Clubhouse. Will your card come in under par? Will you get the hole in one?

- **Nine hand-made holes,** each with its own name, its hazards and its own wind
- **A caddie** who suggests every shot (though he ignores the wind and half-reads the greens); change any of it, or click the map to aim
- **Greens that slope,** bending every putt
- **A scorecard** with par, your strokes and where you stand, and your best round and holes in one, kept between games

## Controls

- **↑ ↓**: club; **← →**: aim (**,** and **.** for fine aim); **+** **-**, **PgUp** **PgDn** or **1** to **0**: swing
- **Enter** or **Space**: swing; a click on the map: aim there
- **C**: ask the caddie again; **N**: a new round; **Tab**: the rules; **M**: sound on or off; **Esc**: quit

## History

A golf game in which you choose the club and how hard to swing was printed in David H. Ahl's *101 BASIC Computer Games* (1973) and again in *BASIC Computer Games* (1978), a whole round played out in words and numbers. This one keeps the club and the swing and lays out the course for you to see, nine holes of our own drawn from above.
