**IT'S TIME FOR YOU TO HEAD TO THE OCEAN.**

Everyone in town says so. Why don't you take a trip to the ocean, they ask; I hear it's wonderful this time of year. There is a boat at the jetty and a river that runs all the way there, through forest and ruin and long, cold nights. You just have to steer it, keep it fuelled, and make it to the inn before dark, because they mostly come at night. Mostly.

Boat Journey is a turn-based roguelike about a voyage down a river to the ocean. You walk the riverside towns on foot and drive the boat through a big generated landscape, turning it in eighths of a circle, as clumsy and as satisfying as steering a real one.

- **Passengers.** Strangers in every town want to go to the ocean too, and each brings a gift, drawn in a hand-made ANSI portrait: the soldier clears a path through the trees with a blast, the surgeon heals your wounds, the thief lets you sneak past your enemies, the physicist lends you an experimental teleporter, the surveyor a telescope, the bitten one scares off other beasts, and the ghost makes you incorporeal for a while
- **Junk.** Pick it up wherever you land and trade it at the inn for fuel and more passenger seats
- **Night.** When the light goes, rest at an inn until morning; outside, beasts hunt you and ghosts drift through the dark, diagonally
- **Endings.** Run out of fuel and make a home aboard the stranded boat. Touch a ghost and watch an unfamiliar figure drive your boat away into the darkness. Or reach the ocean, which welcomes your return, and tell it your name

## Controls

- **Arrows**: walk; walk into a door to open it, into people to talk
- **e**: take the wheel (standing on it) or let go
- At the wheel, **Up** and **Down** drive forward and back, **Left** and **Right** turn
- **1** to **9**: your passengers' abilities; **Space**: wait
- **Escape**: the menu, with save and quit

## Options

- `--rgb`, `--ansi`, `--greyscale`: for terminals without true colour

## History

Stephen Sherratt, who makes games as gridbugs, wrote Boat Journey in seven days for the 7DRL Challenge of March 2023, on chargrid, his own Rust library for games drawn in characters, and drew its character portraits by hand as text art. Text-only graphics, his page says: you can play it in a terminal if you like. It is part of a line of his seven-day roguelikes, one most years since 2018.
