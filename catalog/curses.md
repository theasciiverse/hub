**THAT MAP OF PARIS MUST BE UP HERE SOMEWHERE.**

"It's become a matter of pride now not to give up." It has been five years since your last trip, and the tourist map is lost somewhere in the attics of Meldrew Hall, your family's old house, in among the tea chests and the junk of generations. By the look of it, your great-grandfather was the last person to tidy up. You have a chocolate biscuit, an electric torch with no switch, and a crumpled list of things to do. Number one: find map. Let's face it, it's more enticing than the rest put together.

Then the attic key slips off a glass demijohn and rattles away down a crack in the floorboards, and the search starts turning into something much larger. This family has a history, and a curse to go with it. Aunt Jemima is potting daisies. The house hides ways into other times and places. And you start to wonder whether you are cursed too.

- **Long, witty and famously hard**: a big puzzle game in the grand old style, with a sense of humour all the way through.
- **Literary from the first screen**: lines from Shakespeare and Dorothy Parker turn up as you play.
- **A classic of the form**: Graham Nelson's 1993 game, released as "An Interactive Diversion".

**Find the map. Mind the family curse.**

## How to play

Type what you want to do, in plain words, and press Enter:

- **look**, **inventory** (or **i**), **examine** something (or **x**)
- **north**, **south**, **east**, **west**, **up**, **down** (or **n**, **s**, **e**, **w**, **u**, **d**)
- **take**, **drop**, **open**, **push** things; **help** or **about** in most games
- **save** and **restore**; **quit** to stop

Your saved games are kept in `~/.local/share/curses`.
