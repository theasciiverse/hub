**THE YEAR IS 3000. THE SHIP IS YOURS, AS SOON AS YOU PAY FOR IT.**

The freighter Vindicator answers to you, but she belongs to the bank. You
have 5,000 credits in hand, 8,000 owed, a payment due every single day,
and until the end of the year 3004 to clear the lot. Miss a payment and
the debt grows. Let it run past 30,000 and the bank takes her back.

So you trade. A dozen charted worlds hang on the star map, grassy and
desert, ice, water, swamp and barren, rural backwaters and megacities and
robotic hives, each with its own industry and its own appetite. Fourteen
goods move between them: ore and metals, machinery and consumer
electronics, crops, fertilizer, prepared food, raw fuel, hydrogen fuel and
uranium. The best prices around light up in green, and the news moves
them: a blight on one world's crops, a motherlode of ore struck on
another.

Out past the right half of the map the lanes are lawless. A ship like the
Red Ledger or the Widow's Wake drops out of the dark across your bow and
names its toll. Pay it, burn hard for open space, or turn your plasma
cannon on them: set your crew to their task, close in or evade, fire, and
if their crew falls first you board the wreck and strip it for salvage.

And then there are the worlds marked **?**, off every chart. No market, no
dock, no fuel. Land a survey team and spend three days on the surface,
bringing back samples and salvage worth more the heavier the world, and
sometimes losing people to the gravity or the wilds. On three of those
worlds, in buried chambers, lie the pieces of a Precursor star chart. Put
the three together and they point to the Vault.

- **A ship built from parts:** guns, armour plating, shields, cargo bays,
  berths and engines, bought, sold and repaired at the dock, every one of
  them needing crew
- **A living market:** supply and demand on every world, prices that drift
  day by day, news that shakes them for months
- **Pirates with names:** Black Comet, Gutter Saint, Rust Baron, Cold
  Fortune and their kind, worse on the frontier and keener on a fat purse
- **Ship to ship:** turn-based battles where crew tasks and engine orders
  matter as much as the guns, and fleeing far enough means escape
- **Two ways to win:** clear the debt and fly on as a free trader, or
  follow the fragments to the Precursor Vault
- **Many ways to lose:** the debt, the deadline, a lost crew, or an empty
  fuel tank on a world with no dock

## Controls

- **S**: the ship, **M**: the star map, **T**: trade, **D**: the dock
- On the star map, **j** and **k** pick a world, **Enter** flies there,
  **/** shows prices
- **V**: survey an uncharted world, **H**: hire crew
- In battle, **Space**: next turn, **C**: crew task, **E**: engine (close
  in, evade, flee), **T**: target
- **?**: help for the screen you are on, **Q**: save and quit

## History

Whales, the maker of Cataclysm, started Rogue Star in October 2013 with
a one-line pitch in its README: "Think Star Control 2 / UQM, but more
roguelikelike." Over the following eight months the galaxy filled in:
the Vindicator and her debt, worlds with biomes, populations and
industries, fourteen goods and a market that answers to the news, ships
assembled part by part, reputations as trader, fighter and criminal. A
whole ship-to-ship battle system was written too, but it waited behind a
commented-out test, never reached from the game. In June 2014 the last
commit went in.

In 2026 the Asciiverse took the helm and gave the voyage its stakes: the
debt and its deadline, pirates on the lanes with Whales' battles at last
wired into play, and in the Star Control spirit, uncharted worlds to
survey and a trail of Precursor fragments leading to the Vault.
