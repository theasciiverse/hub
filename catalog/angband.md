Somewhere beneath the fortress of Angband, a hundred levels down, waits
Morgoth, Lord of Darkness. You choose a race and a class, start in a
small town with some gear and a little gold, buy what you can, and go
down. Every level is made afresh each time you enter it, full of
monsters out of Tolkien's Middle-earth and far beyond, traps, treasure,
unique foes and legendary artifacts. Death is permanent, and the game is
long and deep: a win usually takes many characters.

## Controls

- **Arrow keys** or the **number keys** (1 to 9): move; walk into a
  monster to attack it
- **.** and a direction: run along a corridor
- **<** and **>**: take stairs up and down
- **g**: pick up, **i**: inventory, **e**: equipment, **w**: wear or wield
- **q** quaff a potion, **r** read a scroll, **E** eat, **a** aim a wand,
  **u** use a staff, **z** zap a rod, **f** or **v** fire or throw
- **m**: cast a spell, **b**: browse a book
- **o** and **c**: open and close doors, **R**: rest
- **C**: character sheet, **l**: look around, **M**: map of the level
- **=**: options (a roguelike keyset with hjkl is there too)
- **?**: help, **Ctrl-S**: save, **Ctrl-X**: save and quit

## Saving

Your game is saved in your data folder (`~/.local/share/angband`) when you
quit with Ctrl-X, and Angband carries on from it the next time you start.
Character dumps, options and the score list are kept there too.

## History

Angband grew out of Moria, Robert Alan Koeneke's VMS game of 1985, by way
of James E. Wilson's Umoria (1989). Alex Cutler and Andy Astrand wrote
Angband 1.0 at the University of Warwick in 1990; Ben Harrison's rewrite
from 1995, Robert Ruehlmann, Anna Sidwell and the current maintainer, Nick
McConnell, have carried it on, and dozens of other roguelikes are its
variants.
