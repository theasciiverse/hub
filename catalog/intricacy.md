**EVERY LOCK IN THE CITY HAS A FLAW. FIND IT. THEN HIDE YOURS.**

By ruthlessly guarded secret arrangement, the council's agents can pick any lock in the city. The locks come from a secret guild: apparently secure, every one of them with a fatal hidden flaw. And you? You are a candidate for the guild.

A lock is a little machine on a hex grid: blocks held by springs, pivots with arms, loose balls, and a bolt sitting stubbornly in its hole. You work two tools at once. The hook turns and pushes; the wrench, once it gets going, keeps going. Every turn the springs shove and tug back. Nudge the right block, swing the right arm, and the whole contraption shifts, and the bolt slides clear. Then the lock is open, and you know its secret.

Pass your initiation, and the real game begins. Take a three-letter codename. Put up to three locks of your own forward, built to look impregnable and hide a flaw only the cleverest will find. Then turn on your fellow guild members: pick their locks, and prove it with notes on their solutions, kept safe behind your own locks. Every rival you outwit raises your esteem against them, and every lock of yours they crack lowers it. There is no ladder and no throne, only the guild, and whoever is ahead of you in it.

- **Eleven tutorial locks** that teach the tools one idea at a time
- **The initiation**: eighteen locks on a map, from Slippy-slidey and Ratchet to The final challenge, each one opening the way to its neighbours
- **A lock editor** to build your own, with springs, pivots and balls
- **The guild online**: codenames, three lock slots each, notes and esteem, player against player

**The bolt is in the way. Get it out.**

## Controls

- **h**, **l**, **y**, **u**, **b**, **n**: move the hook along the six hex
  directions; **j** and **k** turn it
- **H**, **L**, **Y**, **U**, **B**, **N**: move the wrench
- **x** switches tools, and **a**, **e**, **q**, **w**, **s**, **d** (or
  the number pad) move the one selected; **Space**: wait a turn
- **O**: open the lock once the bolt is clear
- **Backspace** or **X**: undo; **M**: mark a position, **'**: jump back
  to it
- The mouse drags tools too
- **?**: every key in the current screen; **Esc** or **Q**: back and quit

## Online play

The guild meets on Martin Bays' server, which the game connects to by
itself; register a codename there from the guild screen with **R**. The
tutorial, the initiation and the lock editor play offline.

## History

Martin Bays began Intricacy in 2013, in Haskell, as "a game of
competitive puzzle-design": not just puzzles to solve, but puzzles that
players make to beat each other. He worked out its physics of springs,
pivots and forces by long experiment and simplification, and testers,
among them Jason Rohrer and Linley Henzell, helped shape it along the way.
Over the years it gained a single-player initiation, a gentler tutorial
and new locks, and the guild still meets on his server, where players set
and pick each other's locks to this day.
