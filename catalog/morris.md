**THREE IN A ROW TAKES A MAN.**

Place your men one at a time on the points of the board, then slide them along the lines. Three of yours in a row is a mill, and a mill takes one of your opponent's men. Every move either builds a mill, blocks one, or sets up the next. Bring your opponent down to two men, or leave them no move, and you've won.

It's been scratched into Roman bricks, cut into an oak board buried with a Viking ship, written down by a Spanish king in 1283, and it's still played the world over as the mill, merels and morris. Six forms of it:

- **Nine Men's Morris, today's rules**: the tournament game; down to three men, yours fly to any empty point
- **Nine Men's Morris after Bell**: the older game, no flying
- **Morabaraba**: South Africa's game of twelve men, with diagonals at the corners
- **Six Men's Morris**: two squares and six men each
- **Three Men's Morris**: three men each on a small square, where a mill wins outright
- **Three Men's Morris, placing only**: four counters each and no moving

Play the computer at three strengths, from either side, or a friend at the same keyboard.

## Controls

- **Arrows**: move the cursor; **Enter** or **Space**: enter a man, or pick one up and put it down on a lit point; **Esc**: let go, or take back a mill before it bites
- **O**: the carved ornament on or off
- **Mouse**: click a point
- After a mill, the men you may take are lit red: choose one
- **F1** or **?**: the rules and where they come from; **M**: menu; **Q**: quit

## Options

- `--demo`: the computer plays both sides
- `--seed N`: the same game every time
- `--plain`: start without the ornament, for fonts that draw braille thin
