**THE BOARD IS CRAWLING WITH BUGS. SQUASH EVERY LAST ONE.**

Bugs are hiding in the board, letters in reverse video: A, L and S. Two
letters at a time come falling from the top. Steer them, turn them, and
drop them where they count: four of a letter in a line, across or down,
and they vanish, along with every bug caught among them. Whatever was
resting on top falls into the gap, and if it lines up four more, those go
too.

Clear every bug and the next level brings more of them. Every ten pieces
the fall gets a little quicker, and when the next piece has nowhere to
land, the game is over.

Scott Noecker wrote it on a university Unix machine in the summer of 1991
and gave it away, and it travelled the early Linux archives from there.

**Squash them all.**

## Controls

- **Left** and **Right** (or **h** and **l**): move the piece
- **Down** (or **j**): move it down a row
- **Up**, **x** or **s**: turn it clockwise; **z** or **a**: the other way
- **Space**: drop it
- **p**: pause
- **q** or **Esc**: leave the game; **Ctrl+C** quits at once

On the title screen, choose the level (0 to 20, more bugs each) and the
speed (1 to 3) with the arrow keys, then press Enter. Your best score is
kept between games.
