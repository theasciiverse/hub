**FOUR EMPIRES. FORTY PLANETS. NOBODY WAITS FOR A TURN.**

You fly one ship of a team. Federation, Romulan, Klingon or Orion, each
starts in its corner of the galaxy with ten planets, and the war is won
by taking the enemy's. Bomb their armies off a planet, carry
your own across in your hold and beam them down, and do it while their
ships are trying to do the same to you.

A dogfight lasts seconds. Torpedoes have to be led, phasers hit at once
but cost fuel, shields soak up damage until they don't. A game lasts an
hour or more, and nobody wins it alone: a carrier needs escorts, a
planet needs defenders, and a good team talks. Netrek has more in common
with basketball than with an arcade game; the best players are the ones
who think fastest.

Play here against robots that fill the teams and step aside as people
join. Host a game for friends on your network and they join with your
address. Or open the server list and fly with the people who have kept
Netrek alive for decades.

- **Real time, real teams**: up to sixteen ships in one galaxy.
- **Six ships**: scouts, destroyers, cruisers, battleships, assault
  ships, and a starbase to defend.
- **The whole game**: the galaxy map beside the space around you, the
  dashboard, the messages, the player list.
- **Robots built in**: a game whenever you want one.
- **Host or join**: your own server for the local network, or the public
  ones.
- **Sound**: torpedoes, phasers, explosions and the red alert klaxon.

**Pick a side. Take the galaxy.**

## Controls

- **Mouse**: right button steers toward the pointer, left fires a
  torpedo at it, middle a phaser
- **Without a mouse**: Left and Right turn the aim marker, Up steers
  along it, `t` fires a torpedo along it, `p` a phaser
- **Number keys** set speed, `s` shields, `b` bombs, `z` and `x` beam
  armies up and down, `o` orbits
- `h` shows every key, **F2** switches the picture (sextants, Braille,
  half blocks, chafa), **Ctrl-Q** leaves

## History

In 1986 Chris Guthrie and Ed James wrote Xtrek, a space battle game. In
1988 Kevin Smith and Scott Silvey made it Netrek, "a
distributed TCP/IP based xtrek with tons of features", with a server of
its own that players reached over the network. It grew leagues, with
strategies still being invented after years of league play, and players
who have flown it for nearly as long as it has existed.

James Cameron keeps both the COW client and the Vanilla server today,
and new releases still come out. Here they are in the terminal, every
window of the game drawn in characters, with robots ready to play.
