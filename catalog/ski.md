**THE YETI IS GAINING. YOUR SKIS ARE ON BACKWARDS.**

You are skiing down an infinite slope of trees, ice and bare ground, chased by the man-eating Yeti. Unfortunately, you have put your jet-powered skis on backwards, so you can't see where you are going, only where you have been. Each turn adds one line to the slope and asks for your next move.

Hazards grow in patches, so you can steer round them: trees are the worst, bare ground can throw you, ice can make you slip, and you cannot turn while you are on it. When the Yeti comes, jump over him, teleport away, fire an ICBM after him or call up the Fire Demon. Every one of them can go wrong.

- **Many ways to come to grief**: trees, ice, bare ground, the Yeti, a bad landing, your own nuclear backpack and a spell gone wrong
- **A score for every run**: meters skied, jumps made, Yetis melted, minus your injuries
- **Very silly**: in its maintainer's words, "a fun and very silly game that proves you don't need fancy graphical user interfaces to have a good time"

## Controls

Type a command and press **Enter**. Upper or lower case both work.

- **Enter**: keep skiing
- **l** / **r**: turn left or right (each press steers harder, up to five)
- **j**: jump, over trees and Yetis
- **h**: hop, a short jump
- **t**: teleport to somewhere random on the slope
- **i**: launch an ICBM at the Yeti
- **d**: summon the Fire Demon to melt the Yeti
- **?**: help and the map key
- a number in front repeats a command: **5** skis five turns, **3l** turns left three times

On the map, **I** is you, **A** the Yeti, **\*** an ICBM and **D** the Fire Demon; **Y** is a tree, **#** ice and **:** bare ground.

## History

Mark Stevans wrote Ski! for Roy's Games, first in FORTRAN on DEC-10s, then in C for VT100 terminals; version 6.0 is from 1990. Eric S. Raymond took it up in 1993 and rewrote it in Python in 2003, adding the online help and, soon after, repeat counts. This edition is that Python version ported to Rust, so it needs no Python, and asks whether to play again when a run ends.
