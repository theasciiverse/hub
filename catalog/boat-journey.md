**IT'S TIME FOR YOU TO HEAD TO THE OCEAN.**

Everyone in town says so. Why don't you take a trip to the ocean, they ask. I hear it's wonderful this time of year. There is a boat at the jetty and a river that runs all the way there, through forest and ruin and long, cold nights. All you have to do is steer it, keep it fuelled, and reach the inn before dark, because that is when they come out. Mostly.

You walk the riverside towns on foot, then climb aboard and take the wheel. The boat turns an eighth of a circle at a time, heavy and slow and stubborn as a real one, and nosing it round a bend without scraping the bank feels like a small victory every time.

The river is long, and you won't travel it alone. In every town a stranger wants to go to the ocean too, and each of them looks out at you from a portrait drawn by hand in text. Each one brings a gift. The soldier blasts a path through the trees, the surgeon patches your wounds, the thief slips you past whatever is waiting on the bank, the physicist lends you an experimental teleporter, and the ghost, if you dare take one aboard, makes you incorporeal for a while.

Fuel is the other worry. Pick up the junk you find wherever you land and trade it at the inn for fuel and more seats. And when the light goes, stay inside. At night the beasts hunt and the ghosts drift through the dark, always on the diagonal.

There is more than one way for the journey to end. Run dry and make a home aboard the stranded boat. Let a ghost touch you and watch an unfamiliar figure drive your boat away into the dark. Or reach the ocean, which welcomes your return, and tell it your name.

## Controls

- **Arrows**: walk; walk into a door to open it, into people to talk
- **e**: take the wheel (standing on it) or let go
- At the wheel, **Up** and **Down** drive forward and back, **Left** and **Right** turn
- **1** to **9**: your passengers' gifts; **Space**: wait
- **Escape**: the menu, with save and quit

## Options

- `--rgb`, `--ansi`, `--greyscale`: for terminals without true colour

## History

Boat Journey was born in a single week in March 2023, when Stephen Sherratt sat down for the 7DRL Challenge, the yearly contest to make a whole roguelike in seven days. He built it on chargrid, the Rust library he wrote for games drawn in characters, and he drew every passenger's portrait himself, character by character. His page for it says it simply: text-only graphics, and you can play it in a terminal if you like. Here, you can.
