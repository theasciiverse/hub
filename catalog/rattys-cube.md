**TWENTY-SIX CUBIES. FORTY-THREE QUINTILLION WAYS TO MIX THEM UP. ONE WAY HOME!**

A real 3D cube hangs in your terminal, shaded and turning in smooth animation. Reach out with the mouse, grab any sticker and drag: the whole layer swings round under your hand and clicks into place. Drag the empty space around it and the cube turns to show you its far side.

Now press Space. The scramble comes the way the competitions make it, from a truly random position, and then fifteen seconds of inspection, the judge's calls ringing out at eight and at twelve. Your first turn starts the clock. Your last one stops it. Every solve goes into your best time and your averages of five and twelve, so the only rival you need is yesterday's you.

Hopelessly tangled? There's no shame in it. Press **h** and the cube tells you the moves that solve it from exactly where you are. Press **Enter** and sit back while it solves itself, slowly enough to follow, usually in about twenty-one moves.

- **Turn it your way:** drag stickers with the mouse, or type the notation, faces, slices and whole-cube turns
- **A real speedcubing timer** with inspection, +2 and DNF, best, ao5 and ao12
- **Its own solver,** Herbert Kociemba's famous two-phase algorithm, built into the game
- **A flat net** beside the cube, so no face can hide from you
- **Clicks and calls:** every layer clicks home, the judge calls the inspection, and a fanfare greets a new best

How fast can you bring it home? **SCRAMBLE. INSPECT. GO!**

## Controls

- **Mouse**: drag a sticker to turn its layer; drag the background to look around; the wheel zooms
- **R L U D F B**: turn a face clockwise; with **Shift**, the other way
- **M E S**: a middle slice; **x y z**: the whole cube
- **Space**: scramble and start a timed solve; **Esc**: stop the solve
- **Backspace**: undo; **n**: a solved cube to practise on
- **h**: a hint; **Enter**: show me
- **Arrows**: look around; **0**: look home; **+ -**: zoom
- **Tab**: the net; **F8**: sound on or off; **?**: help; **q**: quit

## Options

- `--mute`: start with the sound off

## History

In 1974 Ernő Rubik, a young teacher of design in Budapest, built a cube of small blocks that could twist on every axis without falling apart. The world caught the bug in the 1980s, and it never let go: speedcubers race it in seconds, and in 1992 Herbert Kociemba found his two-phase way for a computer to solve any position in about twenty moves. Ratty's Cube began on 3 November 2026 from a spinning cube example in Orhun Parmaksız's Ratty, a terminal that draws 3D on the graphics card. That cube could only be seen in Ratty, and could only undo its own scramble. So it got a 3D renderer of its own that works in any terminal, a competition timer, and a solver written from Kociemba's own description, and it took its name from the terminal it came from.
