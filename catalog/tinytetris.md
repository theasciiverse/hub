**THE WHOLE GAME FITS ON ONE SCREEN.**

Not the playing field: the program. Conor Taylor squeezed an entire falling-blocks game into 80 columns by 23 lines of C++, and shaped those lines into the game's own name. What comes out when you run it is a clean, quick, honest little game.

Seven shapes drop into a well ten wide and twenty deep, one after another, in seven colours. Turn them, slide them, and slam them down. Fill a row from wall to wall and it vanishes, and the stack above drops into its place. There is no next-piece box, no hold, no levels to hide behind: just the shape in front of you, the gaps below it, and a steady beat that never lets up. Let the stack touch the top and it's over.

- **Pure and fast:** one drop key sends the piece straight to the floor, so every placement is a decision
- **Seven shapes in seven colours,** each one turned and slotted by hand
- **Every line counts:** your score is the rows you clear
- **A best score,** waiting for you next time
- **Ten seconds to learn,** a lot longer to stop playing

**Turn. Slide. Drop. Clear.**

## Controls

- **a** / **d** or **Left** / **Right**: move the piece
- **w** or **Up**: turn it
- **s**, **Down** or **Space**: drop it to the floor
- **q**: quit
- After a game: **r** to play again, **q** to quit

## History

Conor Taylor wrote tinytetris in 2019: a complete falling-blocks game in C++ and curses, minified until it fit in a single 80x23 terminal screen, with its lines arranged to spell out its own name. Beside it he put a commented copy of the same program, so anyone could see how it ticks. Programmers loved it, and it has gathered thousands of stars since.
