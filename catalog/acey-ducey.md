**A SIX AND A QUEEN. FEELING LUCKY?**

You sit down with $100. Two cards come up, aces high. Now bet any amount you like, or pass, that the next card falls strictly between them. Match either one and it's on the edge, which is not between: you lose.

A two and an ace? Push your chips in. A nine and a jack? Only the tens can save you. The table shows exactly how many cards left in the deck would win, and the rest is your nerve. Ride a hot streak, chase a loss, or know when to stand up and walk away with your winnings. Because sooner or later, the table has the last of your money.

- **A ruler of ranks** that lights up every card that would win
- **The odds on the table:** the share of the cards still in the deck that win, before you bet
- **Quick bets:** half, all in, or pass
- **Your record:** games, times broke, your best walk-away and the most you ever held

## Controls

- Type a bet, or step it with **↑ ↓** or the mouse wheel; **Enter** or the Bet button: bet
- **P**: pass; **H**: bet half; **A**: all in; **W**: walk away
- **N**: start over; **Tab**: the rules; **M**: sound on or off; **Esc**: quit

## History

Bill Palmby, a student at Adlai E. Stevenson High School in Prairie View, Illinois, put the old card game In-Between into a program and called it *Acey Ducey*. David H. Ahl printed it in *101 BASIC Computer Games* (1973) and *BASIC Computer Games* (1978); it is the very first game in the 1978 book. Same two cards, same question, and now a deck you can count.
