You are the commander of a Sherman tank and its crew of five, from the
breakout in Normandy in the summer of 1944 to the end of the war in
Europe. Two campaigns: Patton's Best with the US 4th Armored Division
from July 1944, and Canada's Best with the First Canadian Army from
August 1944.

Each combat day is a map of the front to fight your way across, area by
area, calling in artillery and air support and dealing with what you
find. Each encounter is a battle on a hex map round your tank: spot the
enemy, give every crewman his orders, choose the ammunition, and let the
dice decide each to-hit and to-kill roll, all of them shown. Crewmen gain
experience and skills, get wounded, win medals and are killed;
refits bring new tanks; and when your commander falls, his name goes on
the stone on the title screen.

## Controls

- **W/S** or **Up/Down**: choose a crewman; **A/D** or **Left/Right**:
  change his order; **O**: the full list of orders
- **H**: open or close a hatch; **R**: the next shell to load;
  **T**: use the ready rack
- **Space**: on to the next phase
- **Enter**: fire; **Tab**: next target; **A/D**: turn the turret;
  **M**: next machine gun; **F**: area or direct fire with HE
- On the day map: **Tab** and **Enter** to pick an area and move in;
  **W/S** to scroll
- **F1** help with every key, **F2** tank, **F3** crew, **F4** settings,
  **F5** campaign stats, **F7** sound on or off; **Esc**: menu, save and
  quit

## Options

- `--encounter advance`, `battle` or `counterattack`: one encounter with
  a fresh crew, to try the battle rules

## History

Gregory Adam Scott wrote Armoured Commander in Python 2.7 and libtcod
between 2015 and 2017 as free software; Eric Normandeau maintained it
from version 1.04. Scott went on to make a sequel, Armoured Commander II.
This edition ports it to Rust and Ratatui: the same rules, art and
sounds, every screen
laid out for a terminal of 100x30 and growing with a bigger one. Its
README lists the few places where it plays differently.
