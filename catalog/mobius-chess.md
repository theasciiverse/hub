**THE BOARD HAS ONE EDGE. YOUR KING IS ON IT!**

Take a chessboard. Give it a half twist. Join its ends. Now the h-file runs straight on into the a-file, upside down, a1 sits right beside h8, and nothing in fifteen hundred years of chess has prepared you for what happens next!

Möbius Chess is honest chess on that impossible board, drawn as a real twisted strip that turns in 3D beside a flat board showing exactly where everything stands. Every rule you know still holds. It is the world that has changed:

- **Rooks run round the loop:** along a rank, through the join and back along the enemy's rank, 21 squares from anywhere. That rook at the far end of the board? It may be looking straight at your king
- **Bishops turn at the join** and come out on the other colour. The light-squared bishop is not to be trusted
- **Pawns that take across the join** turn round and march home, to promote on their own first rank
- **No corners** to tuck a king into, and **no castling** to save it
- **A starting position found by search:** the usual one hands each side a rook on move one, so every arrangement was tried for one where neither side can take, check or threaten anything on the first move and every pawn is guarded. The rooks wait in the pawn line, and the corners, right on the twist, start empty

Play the computer at three strengths, play a friend at one keyboard, or sit back and watch the computer play itself and learn the strip's dirty tricks. Think you know chess? **THINK AGAIN!**

## Controls

- **Mouse**: click a piece, then where it goes, on the strip or on the flat board; drag the strip to turn it
- **Arrows** and **Enter**: the same with the cursor
- **u**: take back a move; **Esc**: menu (play White or Black, two players, watch, strength)
- **s**: spin on or off; **+ -**: zoom; **m**: sound; **?**: help; **q**: quit

## Options

- `--black`, `--two`, `--watch`: start as Black, two players, or watching
- `--easy`, `--hard`: the computer's strength
- `--mute`: start with the sound off

## History

Chess has been played for some fifteen hundred years, since its forerunner appeared in India. The Möbius strip is younger: in 1858 two German mathematicians, August Ferdinand Möbius and Johann Benedict Listing, each found the surface with only one side and one edge. The two meet in Orhun Parmaksız's Ratty terminal, whose examples include a chessboard on a slowly turning Möbius strip, a sculpture with no rules, while Tony Zeng had written down how chess on such a board would really play. Möbius Chess was built on 3 November 2026 to make it a game: the rules across the join, tested against Zeng's own examples, a computer opponent, and a fair start found by a search through thousands of positions.
