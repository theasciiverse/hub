**FIFTEEN TILES. ONE GAP. PUT IT BACK THE WAY IT WAS.**

The oldest sliding puzzle there is, and still the one that eats an
afternoon. Fifteen tiles sit in a four by four frame, shuffled, with a
single empty space. Slide them back into order, 1 to 9 and then a to f,
with the gap in the bottom right corner. Sounds easy. It's the last three
tiles that get you.

Every tile turns green the moment it is in its right place, so you can
see your progress, and watch it come undone as you move one out of the
way to get another in. Put the cursor on any tile in line with the gap
and press Enter, and the whole line shifts in one move. Or just click.

- **Whole lines at once**: slide a full row or column toward the gap in
  one go
- **Keyboard or mouse**: arrow keys, hjkl, wasd, or a click on the tile
- **Bigger boards**: from three by three up to seven by seven, for
  forty-eight tiles of misery

**"You solved it!" Can you get it to say so?**

## Controls

- **Arrow keys**, **hjkl** or **wasd**: move the cursor
- **Enter**: slide
- **Mouse**: click a tile to slide it
- **F1** or **?**: help on the controls, **F2** or **!**: how to play
- **q** or **Esc**: quit

## Options

Started from a shell, Fifteen takes the size of the board, from 3 to 7:

- `fifteen -s 5`: a five by five board; past 9 the tiles carry letters,
  lower case and then upper case
- `-n`: do not ask to play again after a solved board

## History

Fifteen is one of the games abakh wrote for New BSD Games, a collection of
small curses games started in 2018 and named after the classic BSD games.
The collection is packaged by Debian, Ubuntu, openSUSE, Alpine, Nix,
Homebrew, FreeBSD and pkgsrc, and is in the AUR.
