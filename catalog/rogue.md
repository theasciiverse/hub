The game every roguelike is named after. You are an adventurer at the
top of the Dungeons of Doom, and somewhere below the 20th level lies the
Amulet of Yendor. Each level is a new map of rooms and corridors, drawn
as you explore it, full of monsters, gold, potions, scrolls, wands,
rings, weapons and armour, most of which you will not recognise until
you try them. Death is final; your score is the gold you bring back,
with a tenth off for getting killed.

## Controls

- **hjkl yubn**: move, the last four diagonally; with **Shift** run that
  way, with **Ctrl** run until something is next to you
- **f** or **F** and a direction: fight; **t**: throw, **z**: zap a wand
- **i**: inventory, **,**: pick up, **d**: drop
- **e** eat, **q** quaff, **r** read, **w** wield, **W** wear, **T** take
  off armour, **P** put on and **R** remove a ring
- **s**: search for traps and secret doors, **.**: rest a turn
- `>` and `<`: stairs down and up
- **?**: all the commands, **/**: what a symbol is
- **S**: save and quit, **Q**: quit

## Saving

**S** saves the game to your data folder. To carry on, start Rogue from
a shell as `rogue rogue.save`; a saved game is deleted once it is
restored, as in the original.

## History

Rogue's manual credits Michael C. Toy, Kenneth C. R. C. Arnold and Glenn
Wichman, the authors of the original, together with Timothy Stoehr,
whose rewrite of Rogue 5.3 this is: his November 1987 mail to Kirk
McKusick at Berkeley about this "public domain rogue" is still among its
sources, and it has been part of BSD's games since. Its notice lets
anyone change and share it but not sell it. This edition keeps saves and scores in
your own data folder. It is part of BSD Games in the Asciiverse catalog.
