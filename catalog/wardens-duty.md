**WARDEN, WELCOME TO THE PRISON. ALL OUR CELLS ARE FULL. YOU REALLY NEED TO FIX THAT.**

Every level of this prison is a block of cells, and every cell holds
something that wants out. A goblin. A rat. A carnivorous gelatine. A
mummy, a vampire, a hydra, a Goblin King, a Giant Rat, a dragon. A pangolin
(they say pangolins are dangerous). Step into a cell and the prisoner rises
before you as one huge glyph, staring you down, with your choices beneath
it: a melee attack, a magic missile, an arrow from your bow, or a breath of
fire, water or poison, taken from an elemental prisoner you have beaten.

Every choice has a price, and the gauges show it before you commit: so much
health lost, so much mana spent, arrows used, gold and experience gained.
Fight fire with water and water with fire. Fill the Experience bar and you
grow stronger. Clear every cell and down you go, to a level with more
cells, tougher prisoners and, deep enough, several of them crammed into one
cell together.

You are not alone down there. Some cells hold trapped chests, some hold
nothing but a trap waiting to be set off, and some magic pools waiting to
be drunk from. And a few are occupied by shopkeepers who decided to start
their business in the prison ("Well, laissez-faire, as they say"):
potion vendors, a mana dealer, a skill trainer, and C.M.O.T. Dibbler, who
will sell you almost anything. Even a lutefisk.

There is no way out of this prison. Your duty ends only when a cell holds
something you can no longer beat, so the question is never whether you
win, only how deep you get.

- **A roguelike with no map to wander**: the whole game is in the choices
- **Letters from O.** at every level, each with a P.S. of prison gossip:
  "you should not trust goblins", "eating lutefisk is risky", "roses are
  red, but not in prison"
- **Elemental levels** where fire and water rule, and resistances to train
- **Traps** to spring on purpose: bear traps, falling rocks, fireballs,
  hidden spikes, poison darts
- **Gold** to count at the end, and a deeper level to beat next time

## Controls

- **Arrows**, **WASD** or **HJKL**: move between the cells of a level
- **1** to **9**: pick what to do with the prisoner
- **Enter**: do it; also continues after a message, and starts a new game
  after the last one
- **Ctrl+Q** or **Ctrl+C**: quit

## History

Ondřej Žára, the author of rot.js, made Warden's Duty in seven days for the
Seven Day Roguelike Challenge in March 2015, and turned the genre inside
out: no corridors, no exploring, just one cell after another and the
arithmetic of survival, every creature drawn as a single glyph blown up
large. One of the letters' rumours says it plainly: "He created this game
in seven days." It now plays in the terminal, its scripts exactly as he
wrote them.
