**EVERY MOVE IS A JUMP. RUN OUT OF JUMPS AND YOU LOSE.**

Every hollow of the board starts full, black and white in turn. Two stones are lifted out, and from then on every move is a jump: leap one of your opponent's stones into the empty hollow beyond and take it, and keep jumping along the same line if you dare. The first player who can't jump has lost.

Kōnane was played all across Hawaiʻi on boards cut into lava stone, with pebbles of coral and lava for stones. An officer on Captain Cook's third voyage saw it in 1779 on a board of 238 squares and thought it "much more intricate" than draughts. King Kamehameha, the story goes, sometimes beat his opponent in a single move.

- **Kōnane after Ernst**: the classic, 8 by 8, with jumps that may run on in a straight line
- **Modern Kōnane**: the same, but one jump a move
- **Puʻuhonua o Hōnaunau**: 9 by 11, like the old stone board there
- **King's papamū**: the great board of 238 squares, 14 by 17

Play the computer at three strengths, from either side, or a friend at the same keyboard.

## Controls

- **Arrows**: move the cursor; **Enter** or **Space**: lift a stone at the start, or pick one up and land it on a lit hollow; **Esc**: let go
- **Mouse**: click
- The stones a jump would take turn red when the cursor is on its landing
- **F1** or **?**: the rules and where they come from; **M**: menu; **Q**: quit
- **O**: the braille ornament on or off

## Options

- `--demo`: the computer plays both sides
- `--seed N`: the same game every time
- `--plain`: start without the ornament, for fonts that draw braille thin
