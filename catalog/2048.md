**TWO AND TWO MAKE FOUR. KEEP GOING UNTIL IT'S 2048.**

A 4x4 board and a couple of numbers. Every move slides every tile as far as
it will go, and when two equal numbers run into each other they merge: two
2s make a 4, two 4s make an 8, and on up through 16, 32, 64 and beyond.
After each move a fresh 2 (now and then a 4) drops into an empty square,
and the board fills up.

Keep your biggest tile in a corner. Build towards it, carefully. Then watch
a stray 2 land exactly where you didn't want it. The game ends when the
board is full and nothing can move; every merge on the way adds to your
score.

- **The puzzle that took over 2014**, in Maurits van der Schee's
  single-file C version: no libraries, nothing to set up, straight in
- **Every key you know**: arrows, WASD, vi's hjkl or the number pad
- **Colour schemes** for any terminal, and at game over the final board
  stays on screen for you to admire, or mourn

## Controls

- **Arrow keys**, **WASD**, **h j k l** or **4 8 6 2**: slide the tiles
- **r**: start again (asks first)
- **q**: quit (asks first)

At game over, **r** plays again and **q** quits.

## Options

- `2048 blackwhite`, `2048 whiteblack`, `2048 bluered`: other colour
  schemes for the tiles
- `2048 test`: runs the built-in tests of the sliding rules

## History

Gabriele Cirulli made 2048 in 2014 as an open-source web game, after
earlier games of the same kind. Maurits van der Schee wrote this
console version, 2048.c, the same year, in a single C file that draws
with ANSI escape codes and needs no libraries; it is packaged by Debian
and others. This edition waits at game over so the final board stays
in view, and is built for Windows as well.
