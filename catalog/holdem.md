**SHUFFLE UP AND DEAL. NO LIMIT.**

A thousand chips in front of you, two cards face down, and the blinds at 25 and 50. Peek. Pocket kings? Raise! Seven-two off-suit? Maybe let this one go. Then the flop comes down, three cards for everyone, then the turn, then the river, and every street is another question: check, call, raise to any amount the table can match, or shove the whole stack in and stare them down. Get all-in short and the side pots sort out who wins what at the showdown.

Up to eight computer players sit around the felt, Ada, Bo, Cyd and the rest. They size up their cards against the price of staying in, and never play the same way twice, so you can't read them like a chart. A match is ten hands, and whoever holds the most chips at the end takes it, so one bad beat early on is not the end of the world. Win it back!

- **No-limit Texas Hold'em,** raises to any amount and real side pots
- **Up to eight computer players** who fold the junk and push hard with the goods
- **Two to nine seats,** six to start, and every hand's result laid out on the table

**ALL IN?**

## Controls

- **C**: check or call
- **R**: raise (**Left** / **Right** to choose the amount, **[** / **]** to fine-tune, **Enter** to bet)
- **A**: all in
- **F**: fold
- **Enter**: deal the next hand, when it's yours to deal
- **Esc**: leave (forfeits your chips)
- **Left** / **Right** on the setup screen: how many players; **Enter**: deal; **Q**: quit

## History

Pieczasz began tty.cards in April 2026 with one bold idea: type `ssh tty.cards` and you are sitting at a card table, with no download, no browser and no sign-up form, your SSH key your only ticket in. Over seven months and more than eight hundred commits it grew five games, lobbies, a turn clock and rated tables. Asciiverse dealt each of the five out as a title of its own and filled every table with computer players, so a game is on whenever you are. Hold'em is the high-stakes room.
