**FOURTEEN BATTLES. HISTORY IS WRITTEN. CAN YOU CHANGE IT?**

July 1861, Manassas Junction. Jackson's brigade is holding Henry House Hill, and the whole war lies ahead of you: Shiloh, Antietam, Fredericksburg, Chancellorsville, Gettysburg, Chickamauga, and on to Atlanta in 1864. Fourteen battles of the American Civil War, each fought with the men history brought to it.

You command the Confederate army; the computer, or a friend at the same keyboard, the Union. Before every battle, share out the treasury between food, pay and ammunition while inflation climbs. Feed and pay the men well and they fight; starve them and they desert. Then choose how to fight: a frontal assault, a flanking march, encirclement, an artillery barrage, digging in, guarding the flanks, or falling back to save lives and give up the field.

History's result stands, unless you fight clearly better than history did. Outnumbered more than two to one at Chancellorsville? So was Lee. Win where the South lost, or lose where it won, and the table at the end shows exactly where your war parted from the real one.

- **A briefing for every battle,** with what happened there: the stone wall below Marye's Heights, Pickett's charge, the Bloody Angle, Thomas's stand
- **Morale that matters,** from how well the men are fed and paid
- **Real figures:** men engaged and casualties on both sides, from each battle's record
- **The whole war in a table,** history's result beside yours, battle by battle
- **Two generals:** start with `--two` and a second player commands the North

## Controls

- **Enter**: go on
- **Tab** and **↑ ↓**: move between food, pay and ammunition and change them; typing replaces a number
- **1** to **4**, **↑ ↓** or a click: choose a plan
- **M**: sound on or off; **Esc**: quit

## Options

- `--two`: two generals at one keyboard

## History

In 1968, at Lexington High School in Massachusetts, L. Cram, L. Goodie and D. Hibbard wrote *Civil War*: real battles, real numbers, and a question every history class asks, what if it had gone the other way? G. Paul and R. Hess of TIES made it a game for two, and David H. Ahl printed it in *BASIC Computer Games* (1978). It is rebuilt here with fourteen battles and their figures checked one by one against the record.
