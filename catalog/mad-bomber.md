**It is examination day at The Mad Bomber School of Flying.**

A small miscalculation: the planes have too little fuel to get back to
the airstrip. Fortunately every Mad Bomber plane carries an infinite
supply of bombs, and an idea is hatched. Level the city below and you
can land on what is left. The authorities may not be too impressed with
this strategy, but that can wait.

Your plane crosses the sky from left to right, and every time it runs
off the edge it comes back in one row lower. Below, a skyline of
coloured towers waits. Press **Space** to drop a bomb: it falls straight
down and takes up to three floors off the tower it hits. Only one bomb
can be in the air at a time, so every drop counts.

Clear a path all the way down and the plane lands on the flattened city.
Then comes the next one, with taller towers, and before long a faster
plane. Hit a rooftop on the way down and the exam is over.

Three points for every hit on a standing tower, one for every pass
across the sky. Beat the high score and the school writes your name on
the title screen, for as long as you keep playing.

## Controls

- **Space**: play from the title; in flight, drop a bomb
- **p**: pause; any key carries on
- **q**: give up the flight; at the title, quit
- **i** at the title: the story

## History

Neil James Brown wrote The Mad Bomber in Inform 6 in 1997, his take on
the classic Bomber game. It runs on the Z-machine, the engine built for
Infocom's text adventures, using its timed keyboard input and colours to
fly a plane in real time. The whole game is 3,584 bytes.
