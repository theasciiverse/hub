**IT LEARNS FROM EVERY GAME IT LOSES.**

A three-by-three board, three pawns each. Pawns step straight ahead onto an empty square, or take an enemy pawn diagonally ahead. Reach the far side, take every enemy pawn, or leave the other side with no move, and you win.

You move first, and at the start you will win often: the computer knows only the rules. But every time it loses, it strikes out the move that lost. When every move from a position has been struck out, it resigns. Play enough and it becomes unbeatable.

- **Its memory on screen:** positions met, moves struck out, the run of recent results, and the moves it still trusts from the position in front of it.
- **It remembers between sessions;** R wipes its memory clean.
- **Mouse, arrows, or the number pad.**

## Controls

- **Arrows** and **Enter**, or the mouse: pick a pawn, then its square
- **1** to **9**: a square, laid out like the number pad
- **R**: wipe the computer's memory; **Enter**: play again
- **M**: sound on or off; **Esc**: quit
