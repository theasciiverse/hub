**CHESS BEFORE THE QUEEN.**

This is chess as it was a thousand years ago. The king, knight and rook move as they do today. But the counsellor steps only one square diagonally, the elephant leaps exactly two, and a pawn that reaches the far row becomes a counsellor. No castling, no double step. Win by mate, by stripping away every enemy man but the king, or by leaving the enemy king without a move.

The masters of Baghdad played it at the caliph's court in the 9th and 10th centuries, and wrote it down, with over 1,600 problems that survive to this day. It came from India by way of Persia, and went on to Europe, where in time the counsellor and the elephant became the queen and the bishop.

- **Shatranj**: the Baghdad masters' game; stalemate the enemy shah and you win, and a shah stripped bare can still bare yours straight back for a draw
- **The Medinese victory**: whoever first strips the other's king bare wins at once
- **Chaturanga, the Indian rules**: where a stalemated shah wins
- **The elephant in the corner**: the Indian game, its elephant starting in the corner and leaping two squares straight along the ranks and files
- **Al-Biruni's elephant**: "the trunk and the four feet", one more old elephant

Play the computer at three strengths, from either side, or a friend at the same keyboard.

## Controls

- **Arrows**: move the cursor; **Enter** or **Space**: pick up a man, then put it on a lit square; **Esc**: let go
- **Mouse**: click
- **F1** or **?**: the rules and where they come from; **M**: menu; **Q**: quit
- **O**: the braille ornament on or off

## Options

- `--demo`: the computer plays both sides
- `--seed N`: the same game every time
- `--plain`: start without the ornament, for fonts that draw braille thin
