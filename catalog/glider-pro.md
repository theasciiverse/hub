**THE PAPER GLIDER IS BACK. THE HOUSE GOT BIGGER.**

Three years after Glider, John Calhoun sent his paper glider back indoors,
and this time the house had no end: hundreds of rooms on floor after
floor, joined by stairs, ducts, mailboxes and doors, from the basement to
the roof and out into the sky. You still only ever fall. The air from the
floor vents is what keeps you up, so you drift from vent to vent while the
house does its best to bring you down.

And now the house fights back. Balloons rise, paper 'copters drift down,
darts fly, toast leaps from the toaster, fish from their bowls, drips fall
from the ceiling. Candles and tiki torches burn, outlets spark, shredders
wait, grease spills. Switches bring back the lights or turn on a fan,
triggers set things off a room away, and the telephone rings. Clocks,
stars and folded paper are your rewards; rubber bands, batteries, helium
and a sheet of foil your tools.

Slumberland alone has over 400 rooms. Twenty more houses come with it,
Calhoun's and his players' of the day: the Titanic you have to find the
front of, the Castle o' the Air, SpacePods, Leviathan, Teddy World, the
Land of Illusion and the rest. Two can play, each with a glider of their
own. Leave the title alone and the game plays its own demo, from the keys
Calhoun recorded in 1994.

It is Calhoun's game, translated from his C, with his art, sounds and
houses read from his own files. The silhouette view is made for the
terminal, as in Glider: the room softened behind everything that moves or
hurts, drawn sharp in solid colour. Or press **V** for his art itself, in
half blocks.

**Find the vents. Mind the toaster.**

## Controls

- **Left** and **Right** (or **A** and **D**): fly; let go and the glider
  just falls
- **Down** (or **S**): burn the battery, or rise on helium; **Up**, **W**
  or **Space**: fire a rubber band
- Gamepad: d-pad or stick to fly, **A** fires, **B** or the right trigger
  boosts, **Start** pauses
- **Tab**: pause; **V**: the other view; **Q**: end the game
- On the title: **Enter** play, **2** two players (the second flies with
  **A**, **D**, **S**, **W**), **D** the demo, **L** houses, **T** scores,
  **M** sound, **B** music

Best in a terminal that reports key releases (kitty, foot, WezTerm,
Ghostty and others); two players need one.

## Options

- `glider-pro "Titanic"`: start in a built-in house; `--houses` lists them
- `glider-pro path/to/house`: play a Glider PRO house file (BinHex,
  MacBinary, or the data fork with its resource fork beside it)
- `--demo`: start with the demo
- `--silhouette`, `--original`: which view to start in
- `--mute`: no sound

## History

Calhoun wrote Glider PRO for the Macintosh in C, and Casady & Greene
published it in 1994, three years after Glider 4.0. Where Glider's houses
were rows of rooms, Glider PRO's grew in every direction, and it came with
a house editor of its own. In 2016 Calhoun released its source, art,
sounds and houses under the GPL.
