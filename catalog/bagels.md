**PICO. FERMI. BAGELS. CRACK THE NUMBER!**

The computer is hiding three digits, no digit used twice. You guess, and it answers in its own strange tongue, without ever saying which digit is which:

- **Fermi:** a right digit in the right place
- **Pico:** a right digit in the wrong place
- **Bagels:** not one of your digits is in it

"Pico Fermi" and you are close. "Fermi Fermi Fermi" and you have cracked it. A flat "Bagels" is the best bad news in the game: three digits gone in one go. You have twenty guesses; play it like a codebreaker and eight is plenty. Can you do it in five?

- **A digit pad** that greys out every digit a Bagels has ruled out
- **The count of numbers still possible,** after every clue, so you can watch your logic squeeze the answer out
- **Your record:** solved, missed, your average and your best, kept between games

## Controls

- **0** to **9**, or click the pad: type a guess; **Backspace**: fix it
- **Enter**: guess; **N**: a new number; **Tab**: the rules
- **M**: sound on or off; **Esc**: quit

## History

D. Resek and P. Rowe wrote *Bagels* at the Lawrence Hall of Science in Berkeley, the University of California's science centre for the public. David H. Ahl printed it in *BASIC Computer Games* (1978), a book that sold over a million copies, and its three odd little words went everywhere the book did. Same three words, same twenty guesses, now with a pad that keeps track of what you have ruled out.
