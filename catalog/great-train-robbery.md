**WELL NOW, LISTEN UP, BOYS.**

"Word is there's a train fixin' to roll through our humble little town. But
this ain't no ordinary iron horse, no sir. This one's belly's stuffed to the
brim with glitterin' gold." Why let all that glitter roll on by, when it
could be jinglin' sweet in your own pockets?

You are the brains of this outfit, and the brains do not ride. In the
saloon you hire your gang from the townsfolk, and their names tell you what
you are paying for: Wyatt the Lightning is quick, Silas the Snail is not,
Boone the Tough can take a punch, and Remy the Cheap is cheap. In the
general store you kit them out with revolvers, rifles, a sniper rifle,
dynamite, a rocket launcher if the money stretches, and a regular or a
super-fast horse. In the hotel you choose where each man waits when the
train comes, inside a building or up on a roof, where the range is long and
nobody can reach you, and nobody can get down either.

Then you write their orders, and this is the whole art of it. Your men have
no ideas of their own. Each of them does the first thing on his list that
can be done, and nothing else. Shoot the locomotive to slow the train. Blast
a wagon loose to get at the gold. Deal with the guards who jump out of it.
Collect the bags. Escape. Put "run away" above "shoot", and nobody shoots.

At midnight the train rolls in, and your plan plays out on the town map,
man by man and shot by shot, past the saloon, the hotel and whatever else
this town has built: a bank, a church, a jail, the undertaker's. You cannot
step in. You can only watch, pause
and wince, and then try the same town and the same crew again, knowing
better.

- **A heist you plan, then watch**: no hero to steer, only orders that are
  carried out to the letter
- **A gang with names that tell**: the Lightning and the Snail, the Tough
  and the Sick, the Cheap and the Luxurious
- **Horses, guns and dynamite**, and one rocket launcher nobody in the Old
  West should own
- **A new western town** from every seed: a saloon and a hotel, and maybe a
  bank, a jail, a water tower or a cattle pen
- **The reckoning**: who lived, who died, and how much gold got away

## Controls

- The keys are on screen: **m** the town map, **o** the saloon, **g** the
  general store, **t** the hotel, **k** kick it off, **?** help
- **1** to **0**: pick from a list; the marked letter of an action does it
- **Enter**: confirm; **Escape**: cancel
- **Space**: pause the heist; **A** aborts it while paused
- **Arrows**: scroll the town map when it does not fit the window
- **Ctrl+Q** or **Ctrl+C**: quit

## History

Ondřej Žára, the author of rot.js, made The Great Train Robbery in seven
days for the Seven Day Roguelike Challenge in March 2026, on his own display
and entity libraries, rl-display and FACE. Its manual is refreshingly
honest about it: "Strange, finished in a hurry, not user-friendly." What it
is, is a roguelike turned on its head: you never move a single character,
you only write the plan and watch it come true or fall apart. It came to the
terminal the same year, the game's own script exactly as he wrote it.
