**MULTI-USER CLAY. A WHOLE LIST OF WORLDS, EACH ONE KEY AWAY!**

Somewhere out there, right now, people are casting spells, haggling in taverns and dying in dungeons that have been running since before the web was born. Lera takes you there!

Lera is Swedish for clay, and for mud. Pick a world from the list, press Enter, and you are standing in it: no config language to learn, no host and port to look up. Every MUD in the Asciiverse catalog is already waiting, from Genesis, open since 1989, to the newest worlds, each with what kind of world it is and how long it has been running, and you can add any other world in a few keystrokes.

Once you are in, the screen takes care of itself. Colour comes through as the world's builders painted it, in 16, 256 or millions of colours. Scroll back through the fight you just had and the screen splits, so the live text keeps running underneath and nobody gets a free swing at you. Your health and mana sit in the status line when the MUD sends them, and your password never shows on screen.

- **Every catalog MUD in one list**, filtered as you type, plus worlds of your own
- **Scrollback that splits**, with PageUp or the mouse wheel, so you never miss what is happening now
- **Command history** on Up and Down, and the usual line keys
- **Vitals in the status line** from GMCP, on the MUDs that send them
- **TLS** where the world offers it, and compression to keep things quick
- **A log of every session**, kept per world as plain text, for the adventures worth retelling
- **Reconnect** with one key when the line drops

## Keys

- **Enter**: connect to the world, then send each line
- **Up**, **Down**: earlier commands
- **PageUp**, **PageDown** or the **mouse wheel**: scroll back; **Esc** returns to the live end
- **Shift+drag**: select text to copy
- **Ctrl+R**: reconnect; **Ctrl+D**: disconnect
- **Ctrl+N** in the list: add a world of your own
- **F1**: all the keys; **Ctrl+Q**: quit

From the command line: `lera <world-id>`, or `lera <host> <port> --tls` for anywhere at all.

## History

In 1978 Roy Trubshaw, a student at the University of Essex, started writing a game that many people could play at once, and Richard Bartle carried it on: MUD, the Multi-User Dungeon. Out of it grew thousands of worlds, and some of the ones still open today have been welcoming players for more than thirty years. For most of that time you reached them through bare telnet or a client with a manual to read first. Lera was made in 2026 so that every MUD in the catalog would have a proper home in the terminal: pick, press Enter, play.
