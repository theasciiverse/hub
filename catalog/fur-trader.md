**BEAVER HATS ARE THE RAGE IN EUROPE. GET YOUR FURS TO MARKET!**

The north shore of Lake Ontario, sometime in the 1700s. You have 600 livres, a canoe and eight seasons to make your fortune in the fur trade.

Every autumn, buy your bales of cloth, kettles, knives and beads and hire your voyageurs, three bales to a man. Winter in the upper country with Anishinaabe families at their winter camps, Wendat traders, Haudenosaunee hunters coming in from the hills, or Odawa traders come down by canoe, and trade your goods for beaver, otter, marten, fox and mink. A hard winter with deep snow brings fewer pelts; a good one, more than you hoped for.

Then spring. The ice is out. Where will you sell? Fort Frontenac is close and safe, but it pays the least. Montreal, the great market of New France, pays well, if your canoe makes it down the rapids of the St. Lawrence. Albany on the Hudson pays best of all and sells the cheapest cloth, but it's the longest way, by Oswego, the Great Carrying Place and the Mohawk, where rain on the long carry spoils your pelts and a rival may have got there first. And all the while, ships from Europe bring word: beaver hats are in fashion, or the warehouses are full.

- **A map of the lake** and the three ways to market, with your canoe on its way
- **Storms on the lake, rapids, wet carries, squalls and rival traders,** and the beaver market in Europe swinging prices up and down
- **A ledger, a season log** and your fortune season by season; your best is kept

## Controls

- **Tab** or **↑ ↓**: move between fields; typing or **↑ ↓** sets them
- **1** to **3**, the arrows or a click: choose the post; **Enter**: set out, go on
- **?**: the rules; **M**: sound on or off; **Esc**: quit

## History

Dan G. Bachor of the University of Calgary wrote *Fur Trader*, a trading expedition from Lake Ontario: divide your furs, choose a fort, and take its prices and its risks. David H. Ahl printed it in *101 BASIC Computer Games* (1973) and *BASIC Computer Games* (1978). This one is set among real places of the time: Fort Frontenac, built in 1673 where the lake empties into the St. Lawrence; Montreal, the heart of the trade; and Albany, the old Dutch Fort Orange, English since 1664. The nations of the region appear as what they were to the trade: the trappers, traders and guides it depended on.
