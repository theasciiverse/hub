**CEDAR STATE IS IN THE BUILDING. YOUR CALL, COACH!**

Tip-off! Your Lakeside team against Cedar State, two twenty-minute halves, and five-minute overtimes for as long as it stays level. You don't dribble. You coach. Every time down the floor, you make the call.

With the ball: launch a long jump shot for three, take the short jumper, drive for the layup, try the set shot, or swing it round and wait for an opening, and pray nobody picks it off. Without it: throw on the full-court press, go man-to-man, pack the lane with a zone, or sag off and catch your breath. Every call shifts the odds at both ends.

And the other bench is watching you. Lean on the layup and they'll zone up. Live by the three and they'll smother your shooters. Read them back, mix it up, and steal the game in the last minute.

- **A court that moves:** both teams in formation and the ball in flight
- **Play-by-play** of every steal, foul, free throw, miss and offensive board
- **A coach that adapts:** Cedar State leans against your last call
- **Your season:** wins, losses and your biggest margin of victory

## Controls

- **1** to **5**, **↑ ↓** and **Enter**, or a click: call a play or a defence
- **Enter**: go on after the break and the final whistle
- **N**: a new game; **Tab**: the rules; **M**: sound on or off; **Esc**: quit

## History

Charles Bacheller wrote the basketball game at Dartmouth College in May 1967, the college where BASIC itself was born, and revised it the next spring. In his version you coached Dartmouth, calling a numbered shot on offence and a defence when the other side had the ball. It went out to DEC's users through DECUS and into David H. Ahl's *101 BASIC Computer Games* (1973) and *BASIC Computer Games* (1978). The coaching is the same; now you can see the court.
