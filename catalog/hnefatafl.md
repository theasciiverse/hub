**ONE KING. TWENTY-FOUR VIKINGS WHO WANT A WORD WITH HIM.**

Before chess reached the north, the Vikings played tafl. In the middle of the board sits a king with his small guard. All round the edges wait twice as many attackers, and they would very much like a word with him.

The king wants out. His guards want to get him out. The attackers want to box him in. Every piece slides like a rook, so walls go up and come crashing down fast. Sandwich an enemy between two of yours to take it; the throne and the corners bite too. Get the king to safety and the defenders win. Surround him, and it's over.

The Norse rules are lost, so choose your reconstruction:

- **Copenhagen 11x11**: the tournament game, with shield walls along the edge and the exit fort
- **Fetlar 11x11**: the Shetland rules, no shield wall and no fort
- **Brandubh 7x7**: the quick Irish game, a king and four princes against eight Viking invaders
- **Tablut 9x9, Linnaeus**: written down by Linnaeus in Lapland in 1732, the only tafl game anyone recorded while it was still played
- **Tablut 9x9, Bell 1960**: the same game as an 1811 mistranslation left it, where the king can only be taken from all four sides, and the game shouts Raichi! when he sees one way out, Tuichi! when he sees two. It is not fair, and it is fun

Play the computer at three strengths, from either side, or a friend at the same keyboard.

## Controls

- **Arrows**: move the cursor
- **Enter**: pick up a piece, then put it down on a lit square; **Esc**: drop it
- **Mouse**: click a piece, then a square
- **F1** or **?**: the rules and where they come from; **M**: menu; **Q**: quit
- **O**: the braille ornament on or off

## Options

- `--demo`: the computer plays both sides
- `--seed N`: the same game every time
- `--plain`: start without the ornament, for fonts that draw braille thin
