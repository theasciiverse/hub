**SOMETHING IS HIDING ON THE GRID. COME OUT, COME OUT, WHEREVER YOU ARE!**

Three hunts, one ten by ten field, and a different kind of clue for each. Pick your quarry:

- **Hurkle:** one shy hurkle has crept onto the grid. Guess where, and it tells you which way to look: north, south-west, east. Five guesses to find it, and when you do, it blinks at you and scuttles off. (Halve the field each time and four is enough.)
- **Mugwump:** four mugwumps are hiding, and every guess tells you how far away each one is, to a tenth, but never which is which. Ten guesses to round them all up.
- **Depth Charge:** an enemy submarine lurks in a sea ten levels deep. Every charge tells you which way it lies and whether it is deeper or shallower. Five charges, and if you find it, the sub surfaces and its crew wave a white flag.

Stuck? Press **H** and every spot that still fits the clues is shaded in. Watch the field shrink with each guess as your logic closes the net.

- **Three classic hunts** in one, each with its own clue
- **The shading** that shows exactly what you have ruled out
- **Your finds, misses and fewest guesses,** kept for each hunt

## Controls

- **← → ↑ ↓**: aim; **PgUp** and **PgDn** (or the mouse wheel): set the depth
- **Enter**, **Space** or a click: guess
- **H**: shade what is still possible; **V**: choose another hunt; **Tab**: the rules
- **M**: sound on or off; **Esc**: back to the choice of hunt, then quit

## History

In 1973 the People's Computer Company in Menlo Park, California, printed games in its newspaper for anyone with a terminal. *Mugwump* came from a hide-and-seek program by Bud Valenti's students at Project SOLO in Pittsburgh; *Hurkle*, credited to Bob Albrecht, took its creature's name from Theodore Sturgeon's story "The Hurkle Is a Happy Beast". A year later Dana Noftle sank *Depth Charge* into the very first issue of *Creative Computing*. David H. Ahl gathered all three into *BASIC Computer Games* (1978), and here they are together, one hunt after another.
