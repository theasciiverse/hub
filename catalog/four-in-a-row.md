**DROP. CLACK. FOUR IN A ROW!**

Seven columns, six rows, and a stack of red and yellow discs. Drop yours in at the top and it falls with a wooden clack to the lowest free place, the note rising as the board fills up. Line up four of your own, across, up or on a slant, and a little fanfare plays you out the winner. Your opponent is trying to do exactly the same, and every disc you drop is one they can stand on.

It looks simple. It is not. Make three with both ends open and nothing can stop you. Miss the one they are building on a slant and it's over before you see it coming. And Master is waiting at the top of the ladder, thinking as deep as it can and forgiving nothing. Can you beat it?

- **A computer with four levels.** Easy never misses a win or a block right in front of it, but that is about all. Normal looks five moves ahead, Hard eight. Master does not forgive.
- **Two players at one keyboard,** or with a gamepad each
- **Four boards:** Classic 7 by 6, Wide 8 by 7, Grand 9 by 7, and Five, a 10 by 8 board where it takes five in a row
- **Take it back.** Dropped in the wrong column? Press U.
- **Watch** the computer play itself, and learn its tricks
- **Your record** against every level, kept game after game, and who starts takes turns
- The mouse works: point at a column and click

**ONE MORE GAME...**

## Controls

- **← →** or the mouse: choose a column
- **Space**, **Enter**, **↓** or a click: drop the disc
- **1** to **9**, **0**: drop straight into that column
- **U** or a right click: take your move back
- **Gamepad**: d-pad to choose, A to drop, X to take back, Start for the menu
- **M**: sound on or off, **Esc**: back to the menu, **Q**: quit from the menu

## Options

- `--level easy|normal|hard|master`: the computer's level
- `--board classic|wide|grand|five`: the board
- `--two`: straight into a two-player game
- `--watch`: the computer plays itself
- `--mute`: start with the sound off

## History

Dropping discs into an upright grid has been a family game since the 1970s, and a deeper one than it looks. In 1988 James Dow Allen and Victor Allis, working separately, each proved that on the classic board the player who goes first can always force a win, with perfect play, starting in the middle column. Nobody plays perfectly, and that is the whole fun. Asciiverse made this Four in a Row in 2026 for the terminal, with every clack, fanfare and groan built from square and triangle waves, the sound of the old home computers.
