**A DUKE'S SHARPEST LINES. IN CODE.**

Every letter has been swapped for another, and somewhere under the scramble is a line that has been stinging people for over three hundred years. Your job: find the key.

It's the cryptogram from the Sunday paper. Put the cursor on a letter and type what you think it really is, and every copy of that letter changes at once, so one good guess can light up the whole text. Short words are the way in. Is that lonely letter an A or an I? Does that little word spell THE?

Behind every cipher hides one of the maxims of François de La Rochefoucauld, the 17th century French moralist who saw straight through everybody. "Self-love is the greatest of flatterers." "Hypocrisy is the homage vice pays to virtue." There are 456 of them, in J. Hain Friswell's 1871 translation, with a fresh key every game.

- **Hint mode:** stuck? Press `*` and every letter you've got right turns bold.
- **Any text you like:** started from a shell, `cgram file` scrambles that file instead.
- **The finish line:** get every letter right and the game says it: *solved*.

**CRACK IT.**

## Controls

- **Arrow keys**, or **Ctrl-B**, **Ctrl-F**, **Ctrl-P**, **Ctrl-N**: move
  the cursor
- **A letter**: decode the letter under the cursor as that letter
- **\***: hint mode, which shows correctly decoded letters in bold
- **~**: quit

## Options

Started from a shell, `cgram file` makes a cryptogram of that file's
text instead of a maxim.

## History

David A. Holland wrote cgram around 2004 "for my own amusement", as he
put it when he added it to NetBSD in 2013; Roland Illig reworked it in
2020 and 2021. It takes its text from the fortune program, which the
Asciiverse does not ship, so this edition brings its own collection of
maxims from Project Gutenberg. It is part of BSD Games in the Asciiverse
catalog, under the BSD licence.
