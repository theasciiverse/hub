**ALL RIGHT, BOLD ONE. AS OF NOW, *NONE* HAS EVER EMERGED VICTORIOUSLY. BEWARE!!**

Many cycles ago, in the Kingdom of N'Dic, the gnomic wizard Zot forged his great Orb of Power and vanished, leaving behind his vast subterranean castle: eight levels of rooms, every one dark until you walk into it, filled with esurient monsters, fabulous treasures and, somewhere, the incredible Orb of Zot. Many a bold youth has gone in after it. You are next.

Choose an Elf, a Dwarf, a Human or a Hobbit, share out your strength, intelligence and dexterity, and spend your sixty gold pieces on armour, a weapon, a lamp and flares. Then step through the entrance and watch the map fill in. Every room holds something:

- **Pools** to drink from: you may feel stronger, or dumber, or turn into a Hobbit
- **Books** that teach you dexterity, bore you with Zot's poetry, strike you blind, or glue themselves to your hands
- **Chests** full of gold, or gas, or a bang
- **Crystal orbs** that show you the Orb of Zot, a monster gazing back, yourself in a bloody heap, or a soap opera rerun
- **Warps** and **sinkholes** that throw you across the castle, and **vendors** who sell at hugely inflated prices and all turn on you if you strike one
- **Monsters**: kobolds, orcs, wolves, goblins, ogres, trolls, bears, minotaurs, gargoyles, chimeras, balrogs and dragons. Fight them, run, bribe them with a jewel, or, if you are clever enough, web, fireball or deathspell them. Win, and you may spend an hour eating kobold stew

Step into the wrong empty room and a curse is yours for good: **Lethargy**, **the Leech** that drains your gold, **Forgetfulness** that wipes your map, each held off only by the right treasure. Eight treasures lie in the castle, the Ruby Red and the Opal Eye, the Blue Flame and the Silmaril among them. To win, kill the monster that carries the Runestaff, find the one warp that is the Orb of Zot in disguise, teleport straight into it, and walk back out alive. A stat at zero ends it all, and a run can turn from riches to ruin in three rooms.

## Controls

- **N S W E** or the **arrow keys**: walk; **U** and **D**: take the stairs up or down
- **L**: shine the lamp into a room next door; **F**: light a flare
- **O** (or **R**): open a chest or book; **D** at a pool: drink; **G**: gaze into an orb
- **T**: trade with a vendor, or teleport with the Runestaff (**P** teleports too)
- In a fight: **A** attack, **B** bribe, **C** cast a spell (**W**eb, **F**ireball, **D**eathspell), a direction to run
- **I**: inventory; **H** or **?**: help; **Q**: quit

## Options

- `NO_COLOR=1`: play in black and white

## History

In 1975 Joseph R. Power, a freshman at Michigan State, went to Windycon in Chicago and found printing terminals running a fantasy game called HOBBIT. He got hold of its listing, rewrote it on a TRS-80 at a Radio Shack, and, a Dungeons & Dragons player wanting more than wander, kill and loot, started again from scratch. He typed the new game into the Exidy Sorcerer demo machine at New Dimensions in Computing, a store in East Lansing (the Kingdom of N'Dic is his nod to it), and squeezed it until it fit, hiding the castle in the Sorcerer's character memory. A deal with Kilobaud's software arm fell through, so he sent it to Recreational Computing, which printed Wizard's Castle in its July-August 1980 Fantasy Games issue: some 340 lines of BASIC for readers to type in by hand. It spread to the TRS-80, the Apple II, CP/M machines and far beyond, one of the very first role-playing games on home computers.

Brian "Beej Jorgensen" Hall, of Beej's Guide to Network Programming, has probably played it more than any other game. He wrote down its rules in a detailed spec, rebuilt it as a Rust library in 2018, and in 2019 gave it a full-screen face with the map always in view.
