**EVERY CARD FACE UP. NOWHERE TO HIDE.**

No stock, no hidden pile, no luck of the turn. All fifty-two cards are dealt face up in four rows of thirteen, and the four aces slide to the left end of their rows, leaving four gaps. That's where it starts. A gap takes one card only: the next card of the same suit as its left-hand neighbour. Fill it, and the card you moved leaves a gap of its own, and the chain runs on.

Then the trap. A king closes its row, and nothing may ever sit to its right. Fill the gaps in the wrong order and they die behind the kings, one by one, until nothing can move. Then every card not yet in sequence is swept up and dealt again, and you try once more. Put all four suits in order, ace to king, in as few deals as you can. One to three is good. Eight or more? The deck beat you.

- **One key a move:** every card that can move wears a letter; press it. When only one move is left, the game plays it for you
- **Kings close a row,** so the order you fill the gaps in decides the deal
- **Never more than thirteen deals,** and every one counts against you
- **Real suits:** hearts and diamonds in red, spades and clubs in black, on white cards

**Find the gap. Fill it. Beat the deal.**

## Controls

- **a, b, c, d**: move the card with that letter
- **Any key**: deal again when a deal is over
- **n**: a new game, at the end
- **r**: redraw; **q**: quit

## History

Tim Lister wrote Blue Moon many moons ago in GFA BASIC on his Atari ST ("one of the best versions of BASIC written," as he put it). Somehow it was carried across to C and slipped out onto the Internet without him. Years later he read the code, recognised his own game, and could not say how it had got there. "Ah well," he wrote, "may you live in interesting times." Eric S. Raymond gave it its full-screen curses face in November 1994, and he is still looking after it more than thirty years on, with a new release in 2026.
