**DON'T OPEN! DEAD INSIDE!**

The words are smeared in blood across the airlock door, and you are going through it anyway. The station is out of fuel and sinking towards Earth, and you are its special delivery: one hydrogen fuel cell on your back, five floors down to the fuel bay, and not a word from the crew. Back home your buddies are betting on whether you make it out. Last you heard, the odds were 5 to 1 against. Better make that 6 to 1.

The crew is still aboard. They're just not alive any more. So you shoot your way down, turn by turn, and you learn fast that every gun on a space station is also a hole waiting to happen.

Every weapon has its damage and its penetration, every one of the dead its health and armour. A round that punches through one of them keeps flying into the next, and if it reaches the wall, it can breach the hull. Then a red light comes on, and the air roars out towards the stars, dragging you, your loot and the dead along with it. When the light turns blue, the vacuum has set in, and your oxygen starts counting down.

So you pick your gun for the room. The trusty rifle, the shotgun, a railgun that goes through nearly anything, a chainsaw for close work. Or the strange ones: a gaus cannon that cooks the crew and spares the hull, an oxidiser that turns the dead into air you can breathe, a life stealer that turns them into health. Credits lying about the station buy the upgrades you find, like a strong back to carry a third gun, or a careful aim that halves your chance of breaching.

Find the map of each floor if you can. And when you reach the bottom, there is a twist waiting, and a great deal of paperwork.

## Controls

- **Arrows**, **WASD** or **HJKL**: walk, and aim
- **1** to **3**: fire the gun in that slot; **Escape** cancels the aim
- **Space**: wait; **G**: pick up a weapon; **X**: examine
- **Escape**: the menu, with save and quit, options and help
- A gamepad works too

## Options

- `--rgb`, `--ansi`, `--greyscale`: for terminals without true colour
- `--new-game`: skip the menu; `-r <seed>`: the first run's seed

## History

Stephen Sherratt built Orbital Decay in seven days for the 7DRL Challenge of March 2021, and then refused to stop. Over the next two years he kept polishing it, and made the same game run three ways from one code base: in a window, in a web browser drawn as real text, and in a terminal. This is the terminal one, at home at last.
