The Rogue that Bell Labs built up. You are sent into the Dungeons of
Doom by your fighters' guild to bring back the Amulet of Yendor, as in
Rogue, but there are 52 monsters in both cases of the alphabet, magic
pools that do strange things to what you dip in them, mazes, a Flea
Market, and strength, dexterity, wisdom and constitution to keep up. Two
status lines show them all, with how much you carry.

## Controls

- **hjkl yubn**: move, the last four diagonally; with **Shift** run that
  way
- **f** and a direction: move until something turns up; **t**: throw,
  **p** and a direction: zap a wand that way, **z**: zap
- **i**: inventory, **d**: drop, **a**: your best stats so far
- **e** eat, **q** quaff, **r** read, **w** wield, **W** wear, **T** take
  off armour, **P** put on and **R** remove a ring, **D**: dip something
  in a pool
- **s**: search for traps and secret doors, **.**: rest
- `>`: stairs down
- **?**: all the commands, **/**: what a symbol is
- **S**: save and quit, **Q**: quit

## Saving

**S** saves the game to your data folder. To carry on, start it from a
shell as `srogue -r`; a saved game is deleted once it is restored.

## History

Super-Rogue was made at AT&T Bell Labs between 1982 and 1984 from Rogue
3.6, with Robert D. Kindelberger as the public face of the group; 9.0 is
its last version. Kindelberger gave its source to Nicholas J. Kisseberth
(Yendor) in 2004, and the Roguelike Restoration Project released 9.0-1
in 2005. This edition keeps your files in your data folder and fixes
it for 64-bit machines. It is part of the Roguelike Restoration Project
in the Asciiverse catalog.
