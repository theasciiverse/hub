**THE FACTORIES WANT YOUR ISLAND. THE PEOPLE WANT TO EAT. EIGHT YEARS, PREMIER!**

You are sworn in as premier of Pellora, an island of farms, forest and fishing harbours, five hundred citizens strong. The treasury is full, the air is clean, and foreign companies are already at your door. They want land for factories. They pay well, and they are not clean.

Every year you decide. How much land to sell to industry. How many crowns to spend feeding your people. How many fields to plant. How much to pour into cleaning the air. Sell and the money rolls in, the levies flow and workers arrive from abroad. But the smoke drifts over the island: harvests shrink, the tourists stop coming to the forests, people fall ill, and families pack up and leave.

Push it too far and the assembly will throw you out. Lose too many in one year, let people go hungry while the treasury sits full, or watch the island empty, and your term is over by nightfall. Hold on for all eight years, and the voters decide. Will crowds line the harbour road to cheer you back into office?

- **An island map** of forest, farms and factories, under a haze that thickens with every hectare you sell
- **A chart of your term:** people, money and forest, year by year
- **An election scored out of 100:** citizens kept, forest kept, clean air and money in hand, from a landslide to defeat at the polls
- **Your record:** terms served in full and your best score

## Controls

- **Tab** or **↑ ↓**: move between the orders; typing or the mouse wheel changes a number
- **F**: feed everyone; **P**: plant all you can; **Enter**: end the year
- **?**: the rules; **M**: sound on or off; **Esc**: quit

## History

Around 1970 James A. Storer, a student at Lexington High School in Massachusetts and the same Jim Storer who wrote the lunar landing game, took the idea of *Hamurabi* somewhere new: an island, foreign industry and the pollution it brings. He called it the *Pollution Game*; it went out through DECUS and David H. Ahl printed it as *King* in *101 BASIC Computer Games*. Storer went on to teach computer science at Brandeis. Fifty years on, his question still has no easy answer. Pellora is our own island, and the choice is yours.
