The original Moria. Below a small town of six shops lies a mine that
goes down level after level, each one new every time you enter it, and
somewhere past the 49th level waits the Balrog. Roll a character from
eight races and six classes, buy what you can afford in town, and go
down: fight what you meet, find gold, armour, weapons, potions,
scrolls, wands and staves, most of which you will not know until you
try them, and come back up to spend your gold. Death is final.

## Controls

- **1 to 9** (the number keys or the keypad): move, 5 stays put; **.**
  and a direction runs
- Walk into a monster to fight it
- **o** and a direction: open a door, **c** close, **T** tunnel
- **i** inventory, **e** equipment, **w** wear or wield, **t** take off
- **E** eat, **q** quaff, **r** read, **a** aim a wand, **u** use a
  staff, **f** fire or throw
- **m** cast a spell, **p** pray, **G** learn new spells
- **R** rest, **s** search, **l** look, **C** your character
- `>` and `<`: stairs down and up; walk onto a number in town to shop
- **?**: all the commands, **=**: options, among them the roguelike keys
- **Ctrl-X**: save and quit, **Ctrl-K**: quit for good

## Options

- `-r`: roguelike keys (hjkl to move)
- `-n`: start a new game even if one is saved
- `-d`: show the score table

## Saving

**Ctrl-X** saves your character to your data folder, and starting
Umoria again carries on where you left off. If the terminal is closed
mid-game, the game saves itself first.

## History

Robert A. Koeneke wrote Moria at the University of Oklahoma, after
playing Rogue there, and released it in 1983 for VAX/VMS. James E.
Wilson rewrote it in C for Unix as Umoria from 1987, and Umoria 5.x
became the Moria most people played; it was released under the GPL in
2008. Michael R. Cook restored and modernised it as Umoria 5.7. This
edition keeps saves and scores in your own data folder.
