**THE OLDEST BOARD GAME WE CAN STILL READ THE RULES OF.**

Somewhere around 2600 BC, someone in the city of Ur was buried with their favourite board game. It was dug up in the 1920s, all shell and lapis lazuli, and for most of a century nobody knew how to play it. Then a museum curator found the rules on a clay tablet that had been filed under bird omens.

Two players, seven pieces each, a path of fourteen squares. Throw the lots, move, and pray. Land on an enemy in the shared middle row and it goes all the way back to the start. Land on a rosette and you throw again. That middle rosette has been the most fought-over square on Earth for forty-five centuries.

Pick your rules:

- **Finkel 2017**: the one most people play today
- **British Museum 1990s**: five pieces and no safe rosette, a meaner race
- **R. C. Bell 1960**: a betting game; pieces enter only on a five, and a rosette makes your opponent pay
- **The Babylonian tablet**: five different birds, knucklebones you dare to throw, and every rosette a bet, won by landing and paid for by passing

In the two betting games your purse carries over from game to game. Lose it all and you'll know why the Babylonians kept their accounts in clay.

Play the computer at three strengths, or a friend at the same keyboard.

## Controls

- **Space** or **Enter**: throw
- **Arrows** or **Tab**: choose a piece; **Enter**: move it
- **D**: dare the ox bone (tablet rules)
- **Mouse**: click to throw, then click a lit piece
- **F1** or **?**: the rules and where they come from; **M**: menu; **Q**: quit
- **O**: the braille ornament on or off

## Options

- `--demo`: the computer plays both sides
- `--seed N`: the same throws every time
- `--plain`: start without the ornament, for fonts that draw braille thin
