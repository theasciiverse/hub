**A PIECE EN PRISE IS A PIECE ABOUT TO GET HURT!**

In chess a capture is a quiet thing: a hand, a piece lifted, a little click of wood. Not here. On En Prise's board every capture is a fight, played out on the square while the camera swoops in to watch from the side, and every kind of piece fights its own way:

- **The pawn** levels its spear and jabs, once, twice, and on the third the enemy topples
- **The knight** rears up on its hind legs and crashes down in a ring of dust, and its victim goes flying
- **The bishop** raises his crook and calls down a beam of light that melts the enemy away
- **The rook** lobs stones from its battlements, and the second one never misses
- **The queen** spins on the spot and throws out a ring of light that lifts her prey and breaks it
- **The king** raises his sword high and brings it down once. Once is enough

Underneath it all is honest chess: castling, en passant, promotion with a burst of light, threefold repetition and the fifty-move rule, against a computer at three strengths, a friend at one keyboard, or the computer against itself while you watch the carnage. And because a piece left hanging is a piece about to meet one of the above, any of yours that can be taken for profit glows red. It is **en prise**. Do something about it!

## Controls

- **Mouse**: click a piece, then where it goes; drag the board to turn and tilt it
- **Arrows** and **Enter**: the same with the cursor (up is away from you)
- **Space**: skip a fight; **d**: fights full, quick or off
- **u**: take back a move; **, .**: turn the board; **+ -**: zoom; **0**: look home
- **p**: en prise marks; **m**: sound; **Esc**: menu; **?**: help; **q**: quit

## Options

- `--black`, `--two`, `--watch`: start as Black, two players, or watching
- `--easy`, `--hard`: the computer's strength
- `--quick`, `--no-fights`: fights nearly twice as fast, or none at all
- `--mute`: start with the sound off

## History

In 1988 Interplay's Battle Chess turned the chessboard into a stage, with knights who fought and rooks that became monsters, and a generation of players took pieces just to see what would happen. En Prise brings that idea to the terminal with a cast of its own: figures turned like real chessmen, drawn in 3D by a small renderer two pixels to a character cell, on a board seen from the corner like an isometric game of the same era. The name is the chess term for a piece left open to capture, and the game is about that moment.
