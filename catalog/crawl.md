The big open-source roguelike, still in active development. Pick one of
27 species and 26 backgrounds, go down through the Dungeon and its
branches, collect at least three runes and bring the Orb of Zot back to
the surface. Turn-based, one life per character, no grinding: autoexplore
and autotravel take you through the dull parts, and the game is about the
choices in the dangerous ones. This is the console edition, drawn in text.

## Controls

- **h j k l y u b n**, the arrow keys or the number pad: move, and attack
  by moving into a monster; with **Shift**, run
- **o**: explore the level automatically
- **Tab**: fight the nearest monster
- **5** or **s**: rest or wait
- **g** or **,**: pick up; **i**: inventory
- **w**: wield; **W**: wear armour; **P**: put on jewellery
- **q**: drink; **r**: read; **z**: cast a spell; **a**: abilities
- **f**: fire or throw; **V**: evoke
- **<** and **>**: take the stairs; **G**: travel to a level; **X**: map
- **?**: every command
- **Ctrl-S**: save and quit; your game continues the next time you start

## History

Linley Henzell wrote Linley's Dungeon Crawl in 1997; Brent Ross
maintained it later and released its final alpha, 4.1, in 2005. Since
2006 the Dungeon Crawl Stone Soup team has carried it on, with new
versions ever since, in the open on GitHub. The Asciiverse edition is
the console game, without tiles, with saves, morgue files and init.txt
in your data folder (`~/.local/share/crawl`).
